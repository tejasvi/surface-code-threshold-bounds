\author{Tejasvi Singh Tomar}

\keywords{Quantum error correction, surface code, toric code, threshold, maximum-likelihood decoding, minimum-weight matching, random-bond Ising model, Nishimori line, statistical certificate, computer-assisted proof, Lean}

\begin{abstract}
Under independent bit flips, rigorous lower bounds on the threshold of the surface code from earlier work stay below $0.04$, while numerical estimates place the optimal threshold near $0.109$. We give statistical certificates that the optimal threshold of the planar code is at least $0.100$ under bit flips and at least $0.02$ under phenomenological noise. Each certificate bounds an auxiliary decoder that matches inside windows: a deterministic lemma reduces its failure to rare bad blocks, whose probabilities a pre-registered Monte Carlo experiment bounds. The confidence rests on a binomial model of the counts and on two tested properties of each decoding program. Without sampling, by counting labelled failure paths with exact transfer-matrix certificates, we prove that every minimum-weight decoder has threshold at least $0.0465$ on the planar and rotated codes. All these results are proved in the proof assistant Lean~4, the statistical ones with these conditions as hypotheses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The surface code \cite{Kit03,DKLP02} stores a logical qubit reliably as long as the physical error rate stays below a threshold. In the simplest setting, code capacity, each qubit suffers a bit flip independently with probability $p$ and the syndrome is measured without error. Decoding one error sector is then a problem on a planar graph. For optimal decoding, which picks the most likely logical class given the syndrome, this problem is the random-bond Ising model on the Nishimori line \cite{Nis81,DKLP02}, and the optimal threshold is believed to be the Nishimori point, estimated numerically as $0.10917(3)$ \cite{PPV09}. Minimum-weight matching, the standard efficient decoder, has a threshold near $0.1031$ in simulations \cite{WHP03}.

Rigorous lower bounds are much smaller. Dennis, Kitaev, Landahl and Preskill \cite{DKLP02} showed that minimum-weight decoding, and hence optimal decoding, succeeds with probability tending to one for $p$ below a constant, which their argument puts at $0.0373$. For the toric code, a decoding failure produces a homologically nontrivial cycle on which at least half of the edges are errors, and the expected number of such cycles decays for these $p$. The value $0.0373$ uses a numerical estimate of the connective constant $\mu$ of self-avoiding walks on the square lattice; with the proven bound $\mu\le2.662343$ \cite{Cou22} the same argument gives $0.0366$.
To our knowledge, no larger rigorous code-capacity threshold has been published for either decoder. Other rigorous bounds are lower: a recent method for general local noise channels gives $3.4\%$ for the surface code with bit flips \cite{KM26}, and the bounds for low-density parity-check codes of \cite{DKP15} give about $2.9\%$ for the toric code (see also \cite{KP13}). With measurement errors at the same rate as qubit errors, numerical estimates place the threshold at $0.0293(2)$ for matching \cite{WHP03} and near $0.033$ for optimal decoding \cite{OAIM04}.

On the side of statistical mechanics, Horiguchi and Morita extended the Peierls argument to the $\pm J$ model on the square lattice and showed that a ferromagnetic phase exists when the fraction of antiferromagnetic bonds is below $1-0.97394\approx0.026$ \cite{HM82}. Rigorous results on phase transitions along the Nishimori line in finite dimensions remain scarce \cite{OO26}; for continuous spins in three dimensions see \cite{GS22}. Numerically, the phase boundary of the square-lattice model is reentrant below the Nishimori point \cite{AH04,PPV09}, so the matching threshold lies below the optimal one. Reentrance has been proved, with computer assistance, for a decorated square lattice with independent $\pm J$ couplings \cite{Pei26}.

Thresholds proved for renormalization-group and cellular-automaton decoders are many orders of magnitude smaller \cite{BH11,KuP19}. Under circuit-level noise, the path argument gives $1.7\times10^{-4}$ for the circuit model of \cite{DKLP02}, matching thresholds of $7.4\times10^{-4}$ \cite{Fow12} and $2\times10^{-3}$ \cite{Tom26} have been proved, each for its own circuit and noise model, and the union-find and greedy decoders are known to have finite thresholds \cite{YLY26}.

Critical points have been certified statistically in percolation. The method of Balister, Bollob\'as and Walters \cite{BBW05} turns Monte Carlo estimates into confidence intervals with exactly computed error probabilities, and it gives intervals of width at most $5\times10^{-4}$ at confidence $99.9999\%$ for the site and bond percolation thresholds of the eleven Archimedean lattices \cite{RW07}. We are not aware of a comparable certificate for an error-correction threshold.

\subsection{Results}
Maximum-likelihood decoding of the planar code has threshold at least $0.100$ under independent bit flips (Theorem~\ref{thm:ml}) and at least $0.02$ under phenomenological noise, in which data qubits and syndrome bits fail independently with the same probability (Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}). Both are statistical results: they hold with a confidence, defined in Section~\ref{sec:conf}, that rests on registered Monte Carlo experiments, on a binomial model of their counts and on two tested properties of the decoding programs; their bounds become useful only at distances of order $10^{5}$. Without sampling, every minimum-weight decoder, and hence maximum-likelihood decoding, has threshold at least $0.0465$ on the planar and rotated codes and $0.046$ on the toric code (Theorems~\ref{thm:planar}--\ref{thm:toric}), and $0.016$ on the planar code under phenomenological noise (Theorem~\ref{thm:phenom}), where the argument of \cite{DKLP02} gives $0.0112$, or $0.0114$ with a numerically estimated connective constant. Section~\ref{sec:results} states these results with all constants.

\subsection{Methods}\label{sec:intro-methods}
The argument of \cite{DKLP02} bounds the failure probability by the probability of an increasing event, the presence of a long path on which at least half of the edges are errors. Every argument that counts paths, clusters or witnesses with a union bound does the same, and for matching, Monte Carlo estimates suggest that no such argument reaches $p=0.093$ (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mono}). The certificates leave this class of arguments; the sampling-free bounds stay in it and push it further by exact counting.

For the certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} we bound optimal decoding by an auxiliary local decoder. It divides the code into square blocks. For each block it computes a minimum-weight correction on a window around the block, with edge weights given by the exact marginals of the window posterior, and keeps the part inside the block. A block is bad if the result leaves a defect in it or a long residual path near it; adding errors can remove both, so this event is not increasing. A deterministic lemma (Section~\ref{sec:cert-lemma}) shows that a failure of the decoder forces a sparse hierarchy of bad blocks, which gives an explicit bound on the failure probability once blocks are bad with small enough probability. Under the two tested properties of the program (Section~\ref{sec:conf}), whether a block is bad depends only on the errors near it, and blocks in equivalent positions are bad with the same probability, so only one number per kind of block has to be bounded. Monte Carlo sampling bounds these numbers with exact binomial confidence bounds, in the manner of the percolation certificates mentioned above. Optimal decoding does at least as well as the auxiliary decoder, and the failure probability of optimal decoding is non-decreasing in $p$ and in the width and non-increasing in the distance (Section~\ref{sec:reduction}), so the bound at a single value of $p$, for codes made of whole blocks, gives a threshold.

For the sampling-free bounds (Section~\ref{sec:count}) we fix a failing minimum-weight correction $C^\ast$ that minimizes a linear tie-break functional. Its symmetric difference with the error contains a path between the rough boundaries whose edges are labelled as errors or corrections. Exchanging a short stretch of the path for a geodesic, or for a matching of its endpoints, gives another valid correction; since $C^\ast$ has least weight and, among failing minimizers, least functional, many labelled stretches cannot occur. The remaining labelled walks are counted by a transfer matrix with finite memory, and an integer potential that satisfies every row of the matrix certifies that their growth rate is below one.

Sections~\ref{sec:cert-meas} and~\ref{sec:phenom} carry both methods over to phenomenological noise, on its three-dimensional decoding graph. All theorems of Section~\ref{sec:setting} are proved in Lean~4 \cite{dMU21} with Mathlib \cite{mathlib20} (Section~\ref{sec:lean}), the statistical ones with their conditions as hypotheses; Section~\ref{sec:lean-trust} lists what the formal proofs do not cover.

\section{Setting and main results}\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Decoding graphs}\label{sec:graphs}
All results concern one error sector of a surface code under independent errors, so they are statements about a decoding graph. A \emph{decoding graph} $G$ has a set $V$ of checks, in some cases two boundary vertices $b_1,b_2$ that carry no parity constraint, and a finite set of edges, the error locations; an edge between a check and a boundary vertex is a \emph{half-edge}. Under independent noise of strength $p$, the error $E$ contains each edge independently with probability $p$. The syndrome $\partial E\subseteq V$ is the set of checks of odd degree in $E$. A \emph{correction} is a set $C$ of edges with $\partial C=\partial E$. If $G$ has boundary vertices, $C$ \emph{fails} when $|C\cap\delta(b_1)|$ and $|E\cap\delta(b_1)|$ have different parity, where $\delta(b_1)$ is the set of half-edges at $b_1$; in particular, $E\oplus C$ then contains a path from $b_1$ to $b_2$. For the toric code, $C$ fails when $E\oplus C$ is homologically nontrivial.

We use the following graphs; the rotated code and the toric code are written in coordinates in Section~\ref{sec:count}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The \emph{planar code} $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ of distance $d\ge2$ and width $w\ge1$: checks $(x,y)$ with $0\le x\le w-1$ and $0\le y\le d-2$, an edge between any two checks at Euclidean distance $1$, and a half-edge from each check $(x,0)$ to $b_1$ and from each check $(x,d-2)$ to $b_2$. The bottom and top sides are rough and the left and right sides smooth. For $w=d$ this is one error sector of the unrotated planar code of distance $d$; under independent $X$ and $Z$ errors the other sector is the same graph rotated by a right angle, so for square codes the statements below apply to it by symmetry.
\item The \emph{rotated code} $\mathcal R_d$ of odd distance $d$: the checks are the $X$ checks of the rotated surface code with $d^2$ qubits, and the errors are $Z$ errors. Each qubit is an edge between the two $X$ checks that contain it; a qubit that lies in only one $X$ check is a half-edge to $b_1$ or to $b_2$, according to the rough side on which it lies.
\item The \emph{toric code} $\mathcal T_m$ of side $m$: checks $\mathbb Z_m^2$, each joined to its right and upper neighbour.
\item The \emph{phenomenological graph} $\mathcal Q_{d,w,T}$ of the planar code with $T\ge0$ noisy rounds of syndrome measurement and a final perfect round: vertices $(t,x,y)$ with $0\le t\le T$, $0\le x\le w-1$ and $0\le y\le d-2$, an edge between any two vertices at distance $1$ (space-like edges are data errors, time-like edges are errors of syndrome bits), and half-edges from $(t,x,0)$ to $b_1$ and from $(t,x,d-2)$ to $b_2$. Every edge has the same probability $p$, so data qubits and syndrome bits fail at the same rate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decoders}\label{sec:decoders}
A \emph{decoder} maps each syndrome to a logical class, possibly at random; it fails on $E$ when it outputs a class other than that of $E$. The \emph{maximum-likelihood} (ML) decoder outputs a class of largest posterior probability given the syndrome; it minimizes the failure probability among all decoders, randomized ones included. We write $\PML(G,p)$ for its failure probability. A \emph{minimum-weight decoder} outputs a correction of least size $|C|$. Our counting bounds concern
\[
\PMW(G,p)=\Pr\bigl(\text{some correction of least size fails}\bigr),
\]
so they hold for every rule of breaking ties. Every minimum-weight decoder with a fixed tie rule is a decoder, so $\PML(G,p)\le\PMW(G,p)$. A decoder has threshold at least $p_\ast$ on a family of codes if its failure probability tends to zero as $d\to\infty$ for every $p<p_\ast$, along codes whose width $w$, and for the phenomenological graph also the number $T$ of rounds, are at most polynomial in $d$. All our bounds give exponential decay in $d$ for every $p\le p_\ast$ along such codes.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}
For $0<p\le p_\ast<\tfrac12$ put
\[
\rho(p_\ast,p)=\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{p_\ast(1-p_\ast)}}\ \le 1 .
\]
Theorems~\ref{thm:ml} and~\ref{thm:d1} are statistical: they hold with the confidence defined in Section~\ref{sec:conf}. The other results of this subsection are proved outright. Table~\ref{tab:results} in Section~\ref{sec:lean} lists the Lean theorems.

\begin{theorem}[optimal decoding at $p=0.100$]\label{thm:ml}
Let $L=280$ and $R=L-1=279$ be the block side and the window radius of the registered program. For $w\ge1$ and $d\ge 32L+1$ put $n_x=\max(5,\lceil w/L\rceil)$ and $n_y=\lfloor (d-1)/L\rfloor$. With confidence $1-2\times10^{-9}$, under the conditions \textup{(V6)}, \textup{(Hcov)} and \textup{(Bin)} of Section~\ref{sec:conf}, the following holds: for every $p\in[0,0.100]$, every $w\ge1$ and every $d\ge32L+1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ml}
\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le 422\,n_x\,e^{-0.2155\,(n_y-31)}+2\times10^{4}\,n_x\,e^{-0.02\,(n_y-31)} .
\end{equation}
In particular, under these conditions and with this confidence, the optimal threshold of the planar code under independent bit flips is at least $0.100$.
\end{theorem}

For $n_x=n_y=n$, as for $w=d-1=nL$, the right side of \eqref{eq:ml} is below $0.1$ from $n=986$ and below $10^{-3}$ from $n=1228$, that is, from distance $276{,}081$ and $343{,}841$. For square codes, $w=d$, we have $n_x=n_y+1$, and the two thresholds are $n_y=987$ and $n_y=1228$, that is, distance $276{,}361$ and $343{,}841$. Sharper constants are given in Remark~\ref{rem:sharper}.

\begin{theorem}[planar code]\label{thm:planar}
Let $0<p\le0.0465$, $d\ge11$ and $w\ge1$, and put $\lambda=0.998\,\rho(0.0465,p)$. Then
\[
\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le\PMW(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le w\cdot398.91\cdot\frac{\lambda^{d-11}}{1-\lambda}.
\]\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[rotated code]\label{thm:rotated}
Let $0<p\le0.0465$, let $d\ge11$ be odd, and put $\lambda=0.998\,\rho(0.0465,p)$. Then
\[
\PML(\mathcal R_d,p)\le\PMW(\mathcal R_d,p)\le 2d\cdot366{,}183{,}771.98\cdot\frac{\lambda^{d-11}}{1-\lambda}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[toric code]\label{thm:toric}
Let $0<p\le0.046$ and $m\ge1201$, and put $\lambda=0.997\,\rho(0.046,p)$. Then
\[
\PML(\mathcal T_m,p)\le\PMW(\mathcal T_m,p)\le m^2\cdot428{,}572{,}391.6\cdot\frac{\lambda^{m-10}}{1-\lambda}.
\]
For $0<p\le0.045$ and $m\ge769$ also $\PMW(\mathcal T_m,p)\le m^2\cdot383{,}157{,}811\cdot\lambda'^{\,m-8}/(1-\lambda')$ with $\lambda'=0.996\,\rho(0.045,p)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[measurement errors]\label{thm:phenom}
Let $0<p\le0.016$, $d\ge9$, $w\ge1$ and $T\ge0$, and put $\lambda=0.998\,\rho(0.016,p)$. Then
\[
\PML(\mathcal Q_{d,w,T},p)\le\PMW(\mathcal Q_{d,w,T},p)\le (T+1)\,w\cdot1.2827\cdot\frac{\lambda^{d-9}}{1-\lambda}.
\]\end{theorem}

In each of Theorems~\ref{thm:planar}--\ref{thm:phenom}, $\lambda<1$ for every $p$ in the stated range, so the failure probability decays exponentially in the distance; in Theorem~\ref{thm:phenom} the bound grows linearly in the number $T+1$ of rounds, so the failure probability per round decays exponentially in $d$. The thresholds of minimum-weight decoding and of ML decoding are therefore at least $0.0465$ for the planar and the rotated code, at least $0.046$ for the toric code, and at least $0.016$ for the planar code under phenomenological noise.

\begin{theorem}[optimal decoding with measurement errors at $p=0.02$]\label{thm:d1}
Let $L=35$ be the block side of the registered program of Section~\ref{sec:cert-meas}. For $w\ge1$, $T\ge0$ and $d\ge5L+1$ put $n_x=\max(5,\lceil w/L\rceil)$, $n_t=\max(5,\lceil (T+1)/L\rceil)$ and $n_y=\lfloor (d-1)/L\rfloor$. With confidence $1-10^{-9}$, under the conditions \textup{(V6)}, \textup{(Hcov)} and \textup{(Bin)} for this program (Section~\ref{sec:conf}), the following holds: for every $p\in[0,0.02]$, every $w\ge1$, every $T\ge0$ and every $d\ge5L+1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d1}
\PML(\mathcal Q_{d,w,T},p)\le 804\,n_tn_x\,e^{-0.1008\,(n_y-47)}+5.11\times10^{6}\,n_tn_x\,e^{-0.007\,(n_y-47)} .
\end{equation}
In particular, under these conditions and with this confidence, if $w\ge1$ and $T$ are functions of $d$ that grow subexponentially, that is, $w\,e^{-cd}\to0$ and $T\,e^{-cd}\to0$ as $d\to\infty$ for every $c>0$, then for every $p\in[0,0.02]$ the probability $\PML(\mathcal Q_{d,w,T},p)$ tends to $0$ and is at most $e^{-(d-1)/28000}$ for all large $d$. Hence the optimal threshold of the planar code under phenomenological noise is at least $0.02$.
\end{theorem}

For $n_t=n_x=n_y=n$, as for $T+1=w=d-1=nL$, the right side of \eqref{eq:d1} is below $0.1$ from $n=5018$ and below $10^{-3}$ from $n=5713$, that is, from distance $175{,}631$ and $199{,}956$. These are the constants of the Lean proof, which are not optimized.


\subsection{The confidence statement}\label{sec:conf}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} rests on a fixed program, the first stage of the auxiliary decoder of Section~\ref{sec:cert}, and on a random experiment that was registered before it ran. The bound \eqref{eq:ml} is a deterministic statement $S$. The Lean proof derives $S$ from (V6), (Hcov) and the hypothesis that five unknown numbers $\varepsilon_t$ are at most $5\times10^{-4}$, where $\varepsilon_t$ is the probability that a reference block of type $t\in\{\mathrm{bulk},L0,L1,R0,R1\}$ is bad under independent bit flips at $p=0.100$ (Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp}). The experiment draws $n=1.2\times10^{5}$ independent samples of each type and accepts if, for every type, at most $18$ of them are bad.

\begin{definition}\label{def:conf}
A statement $S$ holds \emph{with confidence $1-\delta$} for a registered experiment if the experiment accepted and
$\Pr(\text{the experiment accepts and } S \text{ is false})\le\delta$,
where the probability is over the randomness of the experiment.
\end{definition}

The conditions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textup{\arabic*}),leftmargin=2em]
\item[(V6)] The first stage of the decoder reads only the syndrome: for every code size and every block, its output on the block depends on the error only through the syndrome in the window of the block.
\item[(Hcov)] The first stage commutes with translations: if a code is embedded in a larger one at an offset by whole blocks, with the sides that a block touches aligned, then the larger code's output on the image of a block is the image of the smaller code's output on the block, for every syndrome.
\item[(Bin)] For each type $t$, the number of bad samples of type $t$ has the binomial law with parameters $n$ and $\varepsilon_t$.
\end{enumerate}
Conditions (V6) and (Hcov) are properties of the registered program. They were tested by the validations V2, V4 and V6 of Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp}, with no mismatch; Table~\ref{tab:conditions} lists, for both certificates, what supports each condition and when it was tested. Validations are finite tests, so (V6) and (Hcov) remain validated hypotheses about the registered program, not proved facts. Condition (Bin) treats the pseudo-random numbers as independent uniform draws. The rounding of the flip probability is handled on paper, outside the formal proof: the sampler compares a uniform number in binary arithmetic with $0.1$, so its flip probability exceeds $0.1$ by less than $8.9\times10^{-17}$, and since a sample uses fewer than $3.92\times10^{6}$ edges, each $\varepsilon_t$ changes by less than $3.5\times10^{-10}$. This is far inside the margin of the acceptance rule: a type passes with probability at most $2\times10^{-10}$ whenever its bad probability is at least $4.99708\times10^{-4}$, so if some $\varepsilon_t>5\times10^{-4}$, the probability that this type passes, and hence that the experiment accepts, is still at most $2\times10^{-10}$. Condition (Bin) also identifies the blocks sampled by the experiment, each in a patch of the code that contains all edges its bad event depends on, with the reference blocks (Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp}). Only the marginal law of each count enters.

The experiment accepted (Table~\ref{tab:cert-counts}). Under (V6), (Hcov) and (Bin), if some $\varepsilon_t>5\times10^{-4}$ then the probability of acceptance is at most $10^{-9}$, and otherwise $S$ holds. A second experiment, with blocks of side $304$ and fresh seeds, was registered during the run under a rule for an interim look that was fixed before the look; it was never run (Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp}). For any rule that reports the bound of an accepting experiment, the probability of reporting a false bound is at most $10^{-9}+10^{-9}$, which is the confidence $1-2\times10^{-9}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml}; here Definition~\ref{def:conf} is applied to the combined registered procedure, both registrations together with the reporting rule. Lean proves both confidence statements from (V6), (Hcov) and (Bin) (Section~\ref{sec:lean-ml}).

\begin{remark}[sharper constants]\label{rem:sharper}
The five exact one-sided Clopper--Pearson upper bounds at level $2\times10^{-10}$ are at most $3.64642\times10^{-4}$ (rounded up; the largest is that of type $R0$), so they are below $\hat\varepsilon=3.6465\times10^{-4}$. By the Bonferroni inequality, all five true probabilities lie below their bounds with probability at least $1-10^{-9}$ for each registered experiment. Evaluating the bound of Section~\ref{sec:cert} at $\hat\varepsilon$ in place of $5\times10^{-4}$, with the level parameter $a_1=12$ that minimizes the bound there, as the registration prescribes, gives
\[
\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le 4.60\times10^{2}\,n_x\,e^{-0.2556\,(n_y-31)}+2.71\times10^{4}\,n_x\,e^{-0.0541\,(n_y-31)}
\]
under the same conditions and with the same confidence $1-2\times10^{-9}$; for $n_x=n_y=n$, and also for square codes with $n_y=n$, it is below $0.1$ from $n=372$ and below $10^{-3}$ from $n=461$. This bound rests on the numerical evaluation of Appendix~\ref{s1:sec:numbers}, which Lean does not check (Section~\ref{sec:lean-ml}).
\end{remark}

Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} rests in the same way on a second registered experiment, with the program of Section~\ref{sec:cert-meas}, whose blocks are cubes of side $L=35$ in the coordinates $(t,x,y)$ of $\mathcal Q_{d,w,T}$ and whose window radius is $R=34$. For this program, (V6) and (Hcov) are the statements above, with translations by whole blocks in each of the three directions, and (Bin) says that for each of its $25$ block types $t$ the number of bad samples has the binomial law with parameters $n=3\times10^{6}$ and $\varepsilon_t$, the probability that the reference block of type $t$ is bad at $p=0.02$; it also identifies the sampled blocks with the reference blocks, as above. The experiment accepts if no type has more than $14$ bad samples. Under (V6), (Hcov) and (Bin), if some $\varepsilon_t>1.85\times10^{-5}$ then the probability of acceptance is at most $10^{-9}$, and otherwise \eqref{eq:d1} holds. The experiment accepted (Table~\ref{tab:cert-meas}), which gives the confidence $1-10^{-9}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}. Its validations of (V6) and (Hcov) at the registered radius ran only after the analysis (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). The flip probability of its sampler exceeds $0.02$ by less than $1.8\times10^{-17}$, which changes each $\varepsilon_t$ by less than $3\times10^{-10}$; a type passes with probability at most $4\times10^{-11}$ whenever its bad probability is at least the Clopper--Pearson bound at $k=14$, which lies more than $10^{-7}$ below $1.85\times10^{-5}$, so this rounding does not affect the confidence either.

\subsection{From the auxiliary decoder to optimal decoding}\label{sec:reduction}
In Section~\ref{sec:cert} we bound the failure probability of an auxiliary decoder at $p=0.100$ on codes made of whole blocks, $\mathcal P_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL}$ with $n_x\ge5$ and $n_y\ge32$, by the right side of \eqref{eq:ml}, under the same conditions and with the same confidence. The first stage of this decoder is the registered program; its later stages are minimum-weight corrections with edge weights in $[0.98,1.02]$. The Lean proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} takes unit weights there, so it has no hypothesis on them; this is allowed because only $\PML$ is bounded, the experiment measures only the first stage, and every such auxiliary decoder bounds $\PML$. Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} follows from this bound, the optimality of ML decoding and the next two lemmas, which hold for every code size.

\begin{lemma}[monotonicity in $p$]\label{lem:monoq}
For $0\le p\le p'\le\tfrac12$, $\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p')$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $p=\tfrac12$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $N$ contain each edge independently with probability $r=(p'-p)/(1-2p)\in[0,\tfrac12]$, independently of $E$. Then $E'=E\oplus N$ contains each edge independently with probability $p(1-r)+(1-p)r=p'$. Consider the randomized decoder that, given $\partial E$, draws $N$, applies the ML decoder for $p'$ to the syndrome $\partial E\oplus\partial N=\partial E'$, and adds the class of $N$ to its output. Since the class of $E'$ is the class of $E$ plus the class of $N$, this decoder fails exactly when the ML decoder for $p'$ fails on $E'$. Its failure probability is therefore $\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p')$, and the ML decoder for $p$ does at least as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[monotonicity in the size]\label{prop:monosize}
For fixed $p\in[0,1]$, $\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)$ is non-increasing in $d$ and non-decreasing in $w$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From $d$ to $d+1$: the checks of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ are the checks of $\mathcal P_{d+1,w}$ in rows $0,\dots,d-2$. Read the vertical edges of $\mathcal P_{d+1,w}$ between rows $d-2$ and $d-1$ as the half-edges of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ at its top side. The restriction of an error of $\mathcal P_{d+1,w}$ to these edges and to the edges below them is an independent error of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$, with the same class, since the class is the parity at $b_1$, and its syndrome is the syndrome of the large code in rows $0,\dots,d-2$. Any decoder of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ therefore gives a decoder of $\mathcal P_{d+1,w}$ with the same failure probability, and ML decoding of $\mathcal P_{d+1,w}$ does at least as well.

From $w$ to $w+1$: a decoder of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ draws an independent error $N$ on the edges of $\mathcal P_{d,w+1}$ that are not edges of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$, that is, the vertical edges and half-edges of column $w$ and the horizontal edges between columns $w-1$ and $w$. From $\partial E$ and $N$ it computes the syndrome of $E\cup N$ in $\mathcal P_{d,w+1}$, applies ML decoding of $\mathcal P_{d,w+1}$ and adds the class of $N$, the parity of its half-edges at $b_1$. It fails exactly when ML decoding of $\mathcal P_{d,w+1}$ fails on $E\cup N$, an independent error of $\mathcal P_{d,w+1}$. Hence $\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w+1},p)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml}]
Let $0\le p\le0.100$, $w\ge1$ and $d\ge32L+1$, and let $n_x,n_y$ be as in the theorem, so that $n_x\ge5$, $n_y\ge32$, $w\le n_xL$ and $d\ge n_yL+1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:monoq} and Proposition~\ref{prop:monosize} give
\[
\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le\PML(\mathcal P_{d,w},0.100)\le\PML(\mathcal P_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL},0.100).
\]
The last probability is at most the failure probability of the auxiliary decoder on $\mathcal P_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL}$ at $p=0.100$, and by Section~\ref{sec:cert} this is at most the right side of \eqref{eq:ml}.
\end{proof}

\section{The certified threshold}\label{sec:cert}

\subsection{Blocks, windows and the auxiliary decoder}\label{sec:cert-decoder}
Fix a block side $L\ge2$ and a window radius $0\le R<L$. We work on codes made of whole blocks, $\mathcal P_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL}$ with $n_x\ge5$ and $n_y\ge4$: the checks are $(x,y)$ with $0\le x<W=n_xL$ and $0\le y<H=n_yL$, the bottom and top sides are rough and the left and right sides smooth. Block $c=(a,b)$, with $0\le a<n_x$ and $0\le b<n_y$, is the set of checks $[aL,aL+L)\times[bL,bL+L)$. An edge between two checks is \emph{owned} by the block of its left or lower endpoint, and a half-edge by the block of its check. Distances between blocks are $\ell_\infty$ distances of their indices, and $N_1(c)$ is the set of blocks at distance at most $1$ from $c$. The \emph{window} $W_c$ is the set of checks at $\ell_\infty$ distance at most $R$ from $c$. The \emph{window graph} $G_c$ has every edge with at least one endpoint in $W_c$; an edge with exactly one endpoint in $W_c$ (a \emph{cut edge}) and every half-edge end at a boundary vertex, so nothing outside the window constrains it.

Let $u$ be a fixed table of independent uniform numbers in $[-1,1]$, indexed by a position modulo $L$ and a direction, generated from a seed fixed in the registration. The edge $e$ with left or lower endpoint $(x,y)$ and direction $\delta$ gets $u_e=u_\delta(x\bmod L,\,y\bmod L)$ and the weight $w_e=1+0.02\,u_e\in[0.98,1.02]$; half-edges are indexed in the same way. Since $u_e$ depends on the position only modulo $L$, translates of a window by whole blocks carry identical weights.

\paragraph{Stage 1.} For each block $c$, the first stage computes from the syndrome in $W_c$ alone a correction $X_c$ of $\cD\cap W_c$ on $G_c$ (Section~\ref{sec:cert-marg}) and keeps the edges of $X_c$ owned by $c$. The union of the kept edges is $C_0$. Its \emph{residual} is $\cD'=\cD\oplus\partial C_0$, and block $c$ is \emph{flagged} if $\cD'$ meets it. Stage~1 is the registered program, and it is the only part of the decoder that the experiment measures.

\paragraph{Levels 0 and 1.} Fix integers $a_0,a_1\ge1$. Let $K$ range over the components of the flagged blocks under adjacency at distance at most $a_0$, and let $R_0(K)$ be the set of blocks within distance $\lceil a_0/2\rceil$ of $K$. If $|\cD'\cap K|$ is even, or $R_0(K)$ contains a check of row $0$ or row $H-1$, or $R_0(K)$ is the whole code, then $K$ is \emph{resolved} by a minimum-weight correction $C_K$ of $\cD'\cap K$ that uses only edges inside $R_0(K)$ and half-edges of its checks. Otherwise $K$ is \emph{odd}, and its blocks pass to level~1, which applies the same rule to them with adjacency $a_1$ and regions within distance $\lceil a_1/2\rceil$.

\paragraph{Top.} Let $\Omega$ be the residual $\cD'$ restricted to the blocks of the unresolved level-1 components. $C_{\mathrm{top}}$ is a minimum-weight edge set of the whole code whose odd checks are exactly $\Omega$ (the boundary vertices absorb any parity). The output is $C=C_0\oplus\bigoplus_KC_K\oplus C_{\mathrm{top}}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cert-valid}
For every syndrome, $C$ is a correction, that is, $\partial C=\cD$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Blocks at distance at most $a$ have $\lceil a/2\rceil$-neighbourhoods that share a block, so the region of a component is a connected set of checks. A resolved component has even residual parity or reaches a rough row, so $C_K$ exists. Each flagged block lies in exactly one component at each level until it is resolved or reaches the top, so the odd checks of $\bigoplus_K C_K\oplus C_{\mathrm{top}}$ are exactly $\cD'$, and $\partial C=\partial C_0\oplus\cD'=\cD$.
\end{proof}

The registered program uses $a_0=4$ and $a_1=20$. The analysis below holds for every admissible $a_1$ at once, because the bad events are events of the first stage, which does not depend on $a_1$; the statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} uses $a_1=12$.

\subsection{The first stage: exact window marginals}\label{sec:cert-marg}
Stage~1 at block $c$ weights each edge of $G_c$ by the log-odds of its posterior probability given the syndrome in the window, and then matches. It has five steps. Write $\cD_c=\cD\cap W_c$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Reference chain.} A fixed function of $\cD_c$ gives an edge set $c_0$ of $G_c$ with $\partial c_0=\cD_c$ on $W_c$: each defect is joined by a straight path to the nearest side of the window rectangle along which every check has a cut edge (ties are broken in a fixed order of the sides); if no side has this property, L-shaped paths are used, and in a window without any cut edge the defects are paired in lexicographic order.
\item \emph{Window posterior.} Identify all boundary vertices of $G_c$ with one vertex $o$, and merge the $m$ edges from each check of the window's perimeter to $o$ (cut edges and half-edges; $m\in\{1,2\}$ in the windows that the analysis uses) into one edge that flips with probability $p_m=\tfrac12(1-(1-2p)^m)$. Under independent flips, the posterior law of the error $X$ on this graph given $\cD_c$ is
\[
\Pi(X)\propto\prod_e p_e^{X_e}(1-p_e)^{1-X_e}\qquad\text{over }X\text{ with }\partial X=\cD_c\text{ on }W_c,
\]
with $p_e=p$ for an ordinary edge and $p_e=p_m$ for a merged edge. Let $P_e=\Pi(e\in X)$; for an edge of a merged pair, $P_e=\tfrac12P_b+(1-P_b)\,y^2/(1+y^2)$ with $y=p/(1-p)$ and $P_b$ the marginal of the merged edge.
\item \emph{Weights.} Each edge of $G_c$ gets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert-weights}
w'_e=\min\Bigl(30,\max\Bigl(-30,\ \ln\frac{1-P_e}{P_e}\Bigr)\Bigr)+10^{-3}\,u_e .
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Matching.} $X_c$ is a minimum-weight correction of $\cD_c$ on $G_c$ for the weights $w'$. Negative weights are allowed and handled by the standard reduction. It is computed by the sparse-blossom implementation of minimum-weight perfect matching~\cite{Hig22,HG25} (based on~\cite{Edm65}), at a version fixed in the registration, on a canonical ordering of the checks and edges of the window; the term $10^{-3}u_e$ separates ties, and the remaining tie behaviour is part of the fixed program.
\item \emph{Commit.} The edges of $X_c$ owned by $c$ are kept.
\end{enumerate}
The construction is designed for (V6) and (Hcov). The reference chain, the weights and the matching are computed from $\cD_c$ alone, so the output on $c$ should depend on the error only through the syndrome in $W_c$; the canonical choice of $c_0$ is what makes this hold bit for bit, since the posterior itself does not depend on $c_0$ but its floating-point evaluation does. The computation depends on the window only through its shape and the periodic weights $u_e$, which is what (Hcov) needs.

\paragraph{The marginals as an Ising model on the dual.} Write $X=c_0\oplus Z$. The set $Z$ ranges over the edge sets with even degree at every check of $W_c$, that is, over the cycles of the planar graph $G_c^{\circ}$ obtained above, and $\Pi(Z)\propto\prod_{e\in Z}y_e$ with $y_e=p_e/(1-p_e)$ for $e\notin c_0$ and $y_e=(1-p_e)/p_e$ for $e\in c_0$. In the plane, the cycles of $G_c^{\circ}$ are exactly the domain walls of the configurations $\pm\sigma$ of spins on its faces. Hence $\Pi$ is the law of the domain walls of the Ising model on the dual graph of $G_c^{\circ}$ with couplings $K_e=-\tfrac12\ln y_e$, that is, with
\[
t_e=\tanh K_e=\pm(1-2p_e),
\]
with the minus sign on the edges of $c_0$: a random-bond Ising model on the Nishimori line~\cite{Nis81}, with bond signs given by $c_0$. By the Kac--Ward formula~\cite{KW52} (equivalently, the Pfaffian method of~\cite{Kas61,Fis61}), its partition function is a constant times $S=\det(I-\Lambda)^{1/2}$, where $\Lambda$ acts on the directed edges of the dual graph, $\Lambda_{\vec e,\vec e'}=t_{e'}\,e^{i\theta(\vec e,\vec e')/2}$ when $\vec e'$ continues $\vec e$ without backtracking, $\theta$ being the turning angle, and $\Lambda_{\vec e,\vec e'}=0$ otherwise. Differentiating,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert-kw}
\langle\sigma\sigma'\rangle_e=t_e+(1-t_e^2)\,\frac{\partial\ln S}{\partial t_e},\qquad
\frac{\partial\ln S}{\partial t_e}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\Bigl((I-\Lambda)^{-1}\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial t_e}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma,\sigma'$ are the spins on the two faces at $e$. Then $\Pi(e\in Z)=\tfrac12(1-\langle\sigma\sigma'\rangle_e)$, and $P_e=\Pi(e\in Z)$ for $e\notin c_0$, $P_e=1-\Pi(e\in Z)$ for $e\in c_0$. The marginals therefore need exactly the entries of $(I-\Lambda)^{-1}$ at the nonzero positions of $\Lambda$.

\paragraph{Computation.} A diagonal unitary similarity, which multiplies the row and the column of each directed edge by a phase determined by its direction, makes $\Lambda$ real with entries $\pm t_{e'}$; it leaves $S$ and the right side of \eqref{eq:cert-kw} unchanged, so all arithmetic is real, in double precision. The matrix $I-\Lambda$ is sparse, with the adjacency of the faces of the window. We order its unknowns by nested dissection~\cite{Geo73}, splitting the rectangle of faces recursively along a line of edges in the middle of its longer side down to pieces of at most $16$ faces, factor it by a multifrontal LU decomposition with dense frontal matrices, and obtain the needed entries of the inverse by the backward recursion of selected inversion~\cite{LLYCE09,LYMLYE11}. For a window with $N$ edges this takes $O(N^{3/2})$ operations and $O(N\log N)$ memory.

The bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} does not use that the marginals are accurate or that the matching is optimal; their accuracy affects only how often blocks are bad, which the experiment measures.

\subsection{Block events and the deterministic analysis}\label{sec:cert-lemma}
Let $F=E\oplus C_0$; its odd checks are $\cD'$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:cert-bad}
Block $c$ is \emph{bad} if it is flagged, or if some path of $F$ inside $N_1(c)$ joins a check of $c$ to a check at $\ell_\infty$ distance at least $L$ from $c$ (the block is \emph{long}).
\end{definition}

By (V6), whether $c$ is bad depends only on the error on the edges with an endpoint in the union of the windows of $N_1(c)$, and these edges lie within $\ell_\infty$ distance $L+R$ of $c$. For two blocks at distance $n$ these edge sets are disjoint when $(n-3)L>2R$. With
\[
D=4+\lfloor 2R/L\rfloor ,
\]
the bad events of any set of blocks with pairwise distances at least $D$ are therefore independent; for $R=L-1$, $D=5$.

\paragraph{Types.} Since $R<L$, the windows of the blocks of $N_1(c)$ reach at most one block column beyond $N_1(c)$. In block rows $2,\dots,n_y-3$, a block in one of the columns $2,\dots,n_x-3$ is of type \emph{bulk}, and the blocks of columns $0$, $1$, $n_x-2$, $n_x-1$ are of the types $L0$, $L1$, $R1$, $R0$, whose windows are clipped by a smooth side. By (Hcov), all blocks of one type are bad with the same probability, that of the reference block of the type (Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp}). The rough rows $0$, $1$, $n_y-2$, $n_y-1$ are never sampled: the analysis declares their blocks bad.

\paragraph{Hypothesis $(\mathrm H_\varepsilon)$.} For every set $S$ of blocks in rows $2,\dots,n_y-3$ with pairwise distances at least $D$, $\Pr(\text{all blocks of }S\text{ are bad})\le\varepsilon^{|S|}$. Under (V6) and (Hcov) this holds with $\varepsilon=\max_t\varepsilon_t$.

\begin{theorem}[failure bound of the auxiliary decoder]\label{thm:cert}
Let $L\ge2$, $0\le R<L$, $D=4+\lfloor 2R/L\rfloor$, $a_0\ge\max(2,D-1)$ and $a_1\ge\max(a_0,2D-2)$ with $a_1$ even, and put $H_0=2a_1+D+2$. Let stage~1 be any program whose output on each block depends only on the syndrome in the block's window, and let the later stages be as in Section~\ref{sec:cert-decoder}, with weights in $[0.98,1.02]$. Assume $(\mathrm H_\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon$ below the convergence limits of the two series of Theorem~\ref{s1:thm:main}. Then there are explicit constants $C_1,s_1,C_2,s_2>0$, which depend only on $\varepsilon$, $D$, $a_0$, $a_1$, the weight ratio $\kappa=1.05$ and $L$, and do not increase with $L$, such that for all $n_x\ge5$ and $n_y>H_0$
\[
\Pr\bigl(\text{the decoder fails on }\mathcal P_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL}\bigr)\le C_1\,n_x\,e^{-s_1(n_y-H_0)}+C_2\,n_x\,e^{-s_2(n_y-H_0)} .
\]
\end{theorem}

In the instance $(\varepsilon,a_1,R)=(5\times10^{-4},12,L-1)$, with $a_0=4$, $D=5$ and $H_0=31$, the bound holds for every $L\ge2$, $n_x\ge5$ and $n_y\ge32$ with
\[
C_1=422,\quad s_1=0.2155,\quad C_2=2\times10^{4},\quad s_2=0.02,
\]
the constants of \eqref{eq:ml}. At the same $(\varepsilon,a_1)$, the numerical evaluation of the series in Appendix~\ref{s1:sec:numbers} gives the sharper second term $9.61\times10^{4}\,n_x\,e^{-0.0351(n_y-31)}$, and at the certified level $\hat\varepsilon$ it gives the constants of Remark~\ref{rem:sharper}.

The proof is given in full in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}; its idea is as follows. Call the components of the bad blocks under adjacency at distance at most $a_0$ the \emph{clusters}. A path of $F$ that starts in a good block $c$ ends before it reaches distance $L$ from $c$, since otherwise $c$ would be long. Hence every component of $F$ meets at most one cluster, and the residual defects, which lie in flagged blocks, lie in clusters. Group the clusters into \emph{families} linked by level-1 components. If the decoder fails, then either the part of $E\oplus C$ attached to a single family has odd parity at both boundary vertices, or the top stage joins a chain of families from one rough side to the other. Parity inside interior clusters forces witnesses, flagged blocks far apart within a cluster or a family, and keeps consecutive families of a chain more than $a_1$ apart; an exchange argument bounds the total weight of the top-stage paths of a chain by the distances between the entry and exit defects within its families, since otherwise re-pairing would lower the weight of the top stage. Among the bad blocks of a cluster, a greedy selection keeps blocks at mutual distance at least $D$, which by $(\mathrm H_\varepsilon)$ are all bad with probability at most $\varepsilon$ to the number of blocks. Counting the two alternatives with exponential tilts then gives two geometric series, and hence the two terms of the bound.

\subsection{The certificate at \texorpdfstring{$p=0.100$}{p = 0.100}}\label{sec:cert-exp}
In the code of $5\times5$ blocks, $\mathcal P_{5L+1,\,5L}$, the blocks $(a,2)$ with $a=0,1,2,3,4$ are the \emph{reference blocks} of the types $L0$, $L1$, bulk, $R1$, $R0$, and $\varepsilon_t$ is the probability that the reference block of type $t$ is bad at $p=0.100$. A sample of type $t$ draws independent flips on a \emph{patch}: the checks within $\ell_\infty$ distance $R+1$ of $N_1(c)$, clipped at the smooth side for the types $L0$, $L1$, $R1$, $R0$, with all edges between them. It then runs stage~1 on the nine windows of $N_1(c)$ and evaluates Definition~\ref{def:cert-bad}. Every edge that the bad event depends on lies in the patch, so a sample is bad with probability $\varepsilon_t$; this identification is part of (Bin).

The registration was frozen before the first sample was drawn. It fixes the program: $p=0.100$, $L=280$, $R=279$, the posterior weights \eqref{eq:cert-weights} on every edge of every window, with their tie term and clipping, the marginal computation of Section~\ref{sec:cert-marg} and the matching implementation at a fixed version; and the later stages, with $a_0=4$, $a_1=20$ and the rule that the statement uses the admissible $a_1$ that minimizes the bound at the certified level. It fixes the experiment: $n=1.2\times10^{5}$ samples of each of the five types, with seeds derived from entropy drawn at the freeze, all of them counted, with no stopping rule. The experiment accepts if $k_t\le18$ for every type $t$, where $k_t$ is the number of bad samples of type $t$. This is equivalent to the exact one-sided Clopper--Pearson~\cite{CP34} upper bound for $\varepsilon_t$ at level $2\times10^{-10}=10^{-9}/5$ being at most $5\times10^{-4}$ for every type: the bound is below $4.99708\times10^{-4}$ at $k=18$ and above $5.1377\times10^{-4}$ at $k=19$. The command of the analysis was written down before the run started, and the block side was chosen before the registration, from pilot runs with separate seeds.

Six validations test the registered program, each with its own random inputs and none reading a certificate sample. V6 compares the stage-1 outputs, matching weights and flags of all nine windows for errors with the same syndrome: $E$, $E\oplus S$ and $E\oplus S\oplus\Gamma$, for plaquette boundaries $S$ inside the windows and a relative cycle $\Gamma$ across them, and the canonical chain of the syndrome; it tests (V6) directly. V2 compares stage-1 outputs, in block coordinates, for errors that are periodic with period $L$, and V4 compares the block events of the sampler with the full decoder on planar codes of $6\times6$ blocks, for all five types; they test (Hcov) and the identification of samples with reference blocks. V1 (two runs of the program on the same samples), V3 (an independent implementation of the marginals and the matching) and V5 (exact transfer-matrix marginals) test the implementation. V2, V4 and V6 first ran with window radius $R=3L/4$, before the run. At the registered radius $R=L-1$ they ran during the run, after the interim look described next had read the counts, and before the analysis. Table~\ref{tab:conditions} gives the outcomes, and Table~R.1 of the records supplement lists every run.

A rule for one interim look, which is not part of the registration, was fixed after the run had started and before any look at the data. It did not allow stopping or changing the running experiment; it prescribed registering a second experiment under a condition on the counts read at the look, and the condition held. The second experiment was registered with $L=304$, $R=303$, the same design, a faster implementation of the same program with bit-identical outputs, and fresh seeds, to be run only if the first experiment had not passed all five types when its machines became free. The first experiment ran unchanged to its registered end and passed, so the second was never run and none of its samples exists. By the union bound of Section~\ref{sec:conf}, the reported bound carries the confidence $1-2\times10^{-9}$ in both branches, including the branch in which the second experiment never runs, although that branch depends on the data of the first; the reported theorem carries the block side $L=280$ of the experiment that passed. Section~R.1 of the records supplement gives the record of the look.

\subsection{The certificate under measurement errors}\label{sec:cert-meas}
Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} rests on a certificate of the same kind on the phenomenological graph. The codes are $\mathcal Q_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL,\,n_tL-1}$ with $n_x,n_t,n_y\ge5$, whose checks form the box $[0,n_tL)\times[0,n_xL)\times[0,n_yL)$ in the coordinates $(t,x,y)$. The blocks are cubes of side $L=35$, and the window of a block is the set of checks at $\ell_\infty$ distance at most $R=34$ from it; distances between blocks are $\ell_\infty$ distances of their indices, so $N_1(c)$ has at most $27$ blocks. Only the sides $y=0$ and $y=n_yL-1$ carry half-edges; the two sides in $x$ and the first and last layers in $t$ behave like smooth sides. Every weight is $1$. Stage~1 computes, for each block, a minimum-weight correction of the syndrome in its window on the graph of the window, in which every check with a neighbour outside the window gets one edge to a boundary vertex; it uses a matching program at a fixed version with a fixed order of nodes and edges, and keeps the edges owned by the block. Levels $0$ and $1$, with $a_0=4$ and $a_1=20$, and the top stage are as in Section~\ref{sec:cert-decoder}. A block is bad if it is flagged or long, as in Definition~\ref{def:cert-bad}, and the bad events of blocks at pairwise distances at least $D=5$ are independent. Outside the declared rows $0$, $1$, $n_y-2$, $n_y-1$, a block has one of $25$ types: one of $L0$, $L1$, bulk, $R1$, $R0$ according to its block column, as in Section~\ref{sec:cert-lemma}, and one of $B0$, $B1$, bulk, $E1$, $E0$ according to its block layer in time, defined in the same way.

The deterministic lemma and the counting of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} have three-dimensional analogues, with $D=5$, $a_0=4$, $a_1=20$, $H_0=47$ and $\kappa=1$, since all weights are equal. Lean proves them in full, for the decoder whose later stages use any minimum-weight corrections for these unit weights: if every type is bad with probability at most $\varepsilon^\ast=1.85\times10^{-5}$, the decoder fails on $\mathcal Q_{n_yL+1,\,n_xL,\,n_tL-1}$ with probability at most the right side of \eqref{eq:d1}, whose rates $0.1008$ and $0.007$ are the tilts of the two counts. Lemma~\ref{lem:monoq} and Proposition~\ref{prop:monosize} hold for $\mathcal Q_{d,w,T}$ with the same proofs; a new layer, together with the time-like edges into it, is added like a new column. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml}, with the optimality of ML decoding, this turns the bound for codes made of whole blocks into \eqref{eq:d1} for all $d\ge5L+1$, $w\ge1$ and $T\ge0$. When $w$ and $T$ grow subexponentially in $d$, so does $n_tn_x$, while the exponentials of \eqref{eq:d1} decay at least like $e^{-(d-1)/5000}$; this gives the last statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:d1}.

\section{Sampling-free bounds}\label{sec:count}

In this section we prove Theorems~\ref{thm:planar}, \ref{thm:rotated} and~\ref{thm:toric} by the scheme outlined in
Section~\ref{sec:intro-methods}. Two features are specific to this section: the smooth sides of the rotated code, which are not
parallel to the lattice axes, and the seam of the tie-break on the torus.

\subsection{Coordinates}\label{cnt:coord}
All three decoding graphs consist of points of $\mathbb Z^2$, or of $\mathbb Z_m^2$, joined by unit steps, together
with half-edges. We write $|u-v|$ for the $\ell^1$ distance, and a lattice path between $u$ and $v$ with $|u-v|$
steps is a \emph{geodesic}. Each half-edge is regarded as the unit step from its check to an \emph{outer point}, a
lattice point outside the graph, as follows.

\emph{Planar code.} For the check $(x',y')$ of $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ in Section~\ref{sec:graphs} we write
$(x,y)=(y',x')$. The checks are then the points of the box $B=[0,d-2]\times[0,w-1]$, each check $(0,y)$ has a
half-edge to $b_1$ with outer point $(-1,y)$, and each check $(d-2,y)$ has a half-edge to $b_2$ with outer point
$(d-1,y)$. These are the coordinates of the Lean statements, which write $n_x=d-1$ and $n_y=w$.

\emph{Rotated code.} The $d^2$ data qubits of $\mathcal R_d$ sit at the points $(a,b)$ with $0\le a,b\le d-1$. For
$0\le i,j\le d-2$ the plaquette with corners $(i,j)$, $(i+1,j)$, $(i,j+1)$, $(i+1,j+1)$ carries an $X$ check when
$i+j$ is even. The weight-two $X$ checks act on the qubits $(i,0),(i+1,0)$ for odd $i$ and on $(i,d-1),(i+1,d-1)$ for
even $i$, $0\le i\le d-2$; we place them at the positions $(i,-1)$ and $(i,d-1)$. The $X$ check at position $(i,j)$
gets the lattice coordinates $(x,y)=\bigl((i+j)/2,(i-j)/2\bigr)$, so that $x+y=i$ and $x-y=j$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item the checks are exactly the lattice points of $R_d=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2: 0\le x+y\le d-2,\ -1\le x-y\le d-1\}$;
\item two checks share a qubit if and only if they are lattice neighbours, and then they share exactly one;
\item the qubits that lie in only one $X$ check are the half-edges. At each check $z$ they correspond one-to-one to the
  unit steps $e$ for which $z+e$ lies beyond one of the \emph{rough lines} $x+y=-1$ and $x+y=d-1$ and inside the
  \emph{smooth range} $-1\le x-y\le d-1$; the outer point of the half-edge is $z+e$. The half-edges across $x+y=-1$
  are the qubits of the column $a=0$ and join $b_1$; those across $x+y=d-1$ are the qubits of the column $a=d-1$ and
  join $b_2$. There are $d$ of each.
\end{enumerate}
These facts follow from the definitions. We also checked them by explicit construction for $d\le21$, and the Lean
development identifies the failure probability of the rotated code with that of this graph
(Section~\ref{sec:lean-count}). A check can have two half-edges to the same boundary vertex; they are different
edges. We call the lines $x-y=-1$ and $x-y=d-1$ the bottom and the top \emph{smooth sides}, and
$h(z)=(d-1)-(x-y)$ the \emph{height} of a point $z=(x,y)$ below the top side.

\emph{Toric code.} We write the checks of $\mathcal T_m$ as $(x,y)$ with $x,y\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}$; each check is
joined to $(x\pm1,y)$ and $(x,y\pm1)$, modulo $m$.

\subsection{Tie-break, failure paths and exclusion rules}\label{cnt:rules}
For the planar and the rotated code let $\phi(x,y)=x+9y$. For the toric code let $\phi(x,y)=x$, the representative in
$\{0,\dots,m-1\}$; this is a function on $\mathbb Z_m^2$, which jumps across the \emph{seam} between the columns $m-1$
and $0$. For a set $S$ of edges let $\Phi(S)$ be the sum of $\phi(u)+\phi(v)$ over the edges $\{u,v\}$ of $S$, where a
half-edge contributes its check and its outer point.

Suppose that some correction of least size fails, and let $C^\ast$ be one with the least value of $\Phi$ among the
failing corrections of least size. The set $E\oplus C^\ast$ has even degree at every check. For the planar and the
rotated code it has odd degree at $b_1$ and at $b_2$, so it contains a simple path $\gamma$ from $b_1$ to $b_2$. Its
first and last edges are half-edges, and its \emph{interior} $\gamma'$, the path without them, joins a check on the
side of $b_1$ to a check on the side of $b_2$. So $\gamma'$ has at least $d-2$ edges: in $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ the
coordinate $x$ runs from $0$ to $d-2$, and in $\mathcal R_d$ every step changes $x+y$ by $\pm1$, from $0$ to $d-2$.
For the toric code $E\oplus C^\ast$ is not null-homologous, so it contains a simple cycle $\gamma$ that is not
null-homologous, and $\gamma$ has at least $m$ edges; we put $\gamma'=\gamma$. In all cases $C^\ast\oplus\gamma$ is a
correction, so $|\gamma\cap E|\ge|\gamma\cap C^\ast|$ because $C^\ast$ has least size. We label the edges of $\gamma$ in
$E$ by $\mathsf E$ and those in $C^\ast$ by $\mathsf C$; every edge of $\gamma$ gets exactly one label.

A \emph{window} is a sub-path $\sigma$ of $\gamma'$ between two checks $u\ne v$. We write $n_E$ and $n_C$ for the
numbers of its labels, $\sigma_E$ and $\sigma_C$ for its sets of $\mathsf E$- and $\mathsf C$-edges, and $T_\sigma$
for the set of checks of odd degree in $\sigma_C$. An \emph{alternative} to a set $S$ of edges is a set $A$ of edges of
the graph with the same checks of odd degree as $S$, such that $A$ has an even number of half-edges at $b_1$ (planar
and rotated code), respectively such that $|S|+|A|<m$ (toric code).

\begin{lemma}\label{cnt:lem-rules}
Let $\sigma$ be a window from $u$ to $v$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $n_C-n_E\le|A|$ for every alternative $A$ to $\sigma$;
\item if $n_C-n_E=|A|$ for an alternative $A$ to $\sigma$, then $\Phi(\sigma_C)\le\Phi(\sigma_E)+\Phi(A)$;
\item $n_C\le|A|$ for every alternative $A$ to $\sigma_C$;
\item if $n_C=|A|$ for an alternative $A$ to $\sigma_C$, then $\Phi(\sigma_C)\le\Phi(A)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For an alternative $A$ to $\sigma$ put $C'=C^\ast\oplus\sigma\oplus A$. It is a correction, since $\sigma\oplus A$ has
even degree at every check, and $|C'|=|C^\ast|-n_C+n_E+|A|-2k$, where $k$ is the number of edges of $A$ in
$(C^\ast\setminus\sigma_C)\cup\sigma_E$. It fails, because $C'\oplus C^\ast=\sigma\oplus A$ has an even number of
half-edges at $b_1$, respectively has fewer than $m$ edges and is therefore null-homologous. Since $C^\ast$ has least
size, (i) follows. In the case of (ii), $k=0$, so $C'$ is a failing correction of least size; it is the disjoint union
of $C^\ast\setminus\sigma_C$, $\sigma_E$ and $A$, so $\Phi(C')=\Phi(C^\ast)-\Phi(\sigma_C)+\Phi(\sigma_E)+\Phi(A)$, and
the choice of $C^\ast$ gives (ii). Parts (iii) and (iv) follow in the same way from $C''=C^\ast\oplus\sigma_C\oplus A$,
with $|C''|=|C^\ast|-n_C+|A|-2k''$, where $k''$ is the number of edges of $A$ in $C^\ast\setminus\sigma_C$.
\end{proof}

The certificates apply Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-rules} with explicit alternatives: in (i) and (ii) a geodesic from $u$ to $v$,
and in (iii) and (iv) the union of geodesics that join the pairs of a perfect matching of $T_\sigma$ of least total
length $c(T_\sigma)$. If these geodesics overlap, the edges used an odd number of times form an alternative with fewer
than $c(T_\sigma)$ edges; so when $n_C=c(T_\sigma)$, (iii) forces them to be disjoint, and (iv) applies to their union.
In (ii) and (iv) the strongest test uses geodesics of least $\Phi$.
For $\phi=x+9y$ the least $\Phi$ among all lattice geodesics from $u$ to $v$ is attained by the \emph{canonical
geodesic}, which takes its steps in increasing order of their $\phi$-increments, that is, in the order $-y,-x,+x,+y$:
exchanging two consecutive steps whose increments are in the wrong order lowers $\Phi$. Two consecutive
$\mathsf C$-edges that turn a corner form a window with $n_C=2=|u-v|$, so by (ii) they turn from the smaller to the
larger $\phi$-increment whenever the other corner is available. We say that a window \emph{fails a test} if (i)--(iv)
are violated for one of these edge sets. By Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-rules}, no window of $\gamma'$ fails a test, as long as
every edge set used in a test is an alternative. Whether it is depends on the graph.

\subsection{The tests in each graph}\label{cnt:regions}

\emph{Planar code.} Every lattice geodesic between two points of the box $B$ stays in $B$. So every geodesic between
checks of a window is an alternative (it has no half-edge), and the tests use all lattice geodesics, with no condition
on the position of the window.

\emph{Rotated code.} Two lemmas make the tests valid in $R_d$. The first handles the rough sides.

\begin{lemma}\label{cnt:lem-rough}
Let $P$ be a set of unit steps forming paths between checks of $R_d$, all of whose points lie in the smooth range
$-1\le x-y\le d-1$. Then $\mathcal R_d$ contains a set $A$ of edges with the same checks of odd degree as $P$ and an
even number of half-edges at $b_1$ and at $b_2$, such that $|A|\le|P|$, and $\Phi(A)=\Phi(P)$ if $|A|=|P|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A point of $P$ outside $R_d$ lies beyond a rough line. A maximal stretch of a path of $P$ outside $R_d$ leaves $R_d$ by
a step from a check $z$ to a point $t$ and returns by a step from a point $t'$ to a check $z'$; it stays beyond one
rough line, since every step changes $x+y$ by $\pm1$. The steps $z\to t$ and $t'\to z'$ cross this line and end in the
smooth range, so by (c) they are half-edges of $z$ and of $z'$ to the same boundary vertex, with outer points $t$ and
$t'$. Replace each such stretch by these two half-edges, and let $A$ be the set of edges used an odd number of times.
A stretch has at least two steps, and exactly two when $t=t'$; then the two half-edges have the same value of $\Phi$
as the two steps they replace. So $|A|\le|P|$, with equality only if every stretch has two steps and no edge is used
twice, in which case $\Phi(A)=\Phi(P)$. Half-edges are added in pairs at one boundary vertex.
\end{proof}

The second handles the smooth sides. Write $\xi=x-y$.

\begin{lemma}[band lemma]\label{cnt:lem-band}
Let $a,b\in\mathbb Z^2$, let $c\ge\max(\xi(a),\xi(b))$, and let $P$ be a geodesic from $a$ to $b$ of least $\Phi$
among the geodesics all of whose points satisfy $\xi\le c$. Then every point of $P$ satisfies
$\xi\ge\min\bigl(\xi(a),\xi(b),c-1\bigr)$. Without the constraint $\xi\le c$, every point of the canonical geodesic
satisfies $\xi\ge\min(\xi(a),\xi(b))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A geodesic uses one horizontal and one vertical direction. If its profile $\xi$ is not monotone, the two directions
are $+x,+y$ or $-x,-y$. In both cases one of them, $s_1$, raises $\xi$ by $1$ and has the smaller $\phi$-increment
($+x$ against $+y$, and $-y$ against $-x$), while the other, $s_2$, lowers $\xi$ by $1$. Exchanging two consecutive
steps $s_2,s_1$ into $s_1,s_2$ lowers $\Phi$ by $2(\phi(s_2)-\phi(s_1))>0$ and raises the point between them by $2$ in
$\xi$. Suppose that the least value $l$ of $\xi$ on $P$ is below $\min(\xi(a),\xi(b),c-1)$. At the first point of $P$
with $\xi=l$, the path arrives by a step $s_2$ and leaves by a step $s_1$, from a point at level $l+1\le c-1$; the
exchange keeps the path at $\xi\le c$ and lowers $\Phi$, a contradiction. Without the constraint the same exchange
applies at every level, and the canonical geodesic is the unique geodesic of least $\Phi$.
\end{proof}

For a window $\sigma$ let $\xi_\sigma$ be the least value of $\xi$ on its points. The points of $\sigma$ lie in
$R_d$, so $\xi_\sigma\ge-1$, and since consecutive points differ by $1$ in $\xi$ and all satisfy $\xi\le d-1$, also
$\xi_\sigma\le d-2$. The tests for $\mathcal R_d$ use, for each pair of checks of the window, a geodesic of least
$\Phi$ among those whose points have height $h\ge0$, that is, $\xi\le c=d-1$; in (iii) and (iv) these are taken over
the pairs of a least-length matching. By Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-band} and $\xi_\sigma\le d-2$, every such geodesic stays at
$\xi\ge\xi_\sigma\ge-1$, inside the smooth range. Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-rough} turns it, or the union of the matching
geodesics, into an edge set $A$ of $\mathcal R_d$ with no more edges. If $A$ has fewer edges, the window violates (i)
or (iii) for the alternative $A$; otherwise $\Phi(A)$ equals the $\Phi$ of the geodesics. In both cases a window that
fails a test violates Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-rules}. This holds for every $d$.

The tests of a window depend on its position only through the ceiling $\xi\le d-1$. A geodesic between two points
$a,b$ of a window rises at most $|a-b|\le M$ above $\max(\xi(a),\xi(b))$, where $M$ bounds the number of edges of the
windows we test. So if all points of a window have height at least $M$, the ceiling does not bind, and the tests are
those of the planar code (all lattice geodesics).

\emph{Toric code.} The windows we test have at most $M$ edges, and every alternative used has at most $M$ edges, with
$2M<m$. So $|\sigma|+|A|<m$ for these edge sets, and the tests use all lattice geodesics on the torus, compared by the
true $\Phi$. Measure the position of a point by its \emph{offset} $o$ from the seam, the representative of its column
in $\{-\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\dots,\lceil m/2\rceil-1\}$, so that $\phi=o$ for $o\ge0$ and $\phi=m+o$ for $o<0$. A window
and its alternatives lie in the range of offsets spanned by the window. If this range does not contain both $-1$ and
$0$, the window lies on one side of the seam, $\phi$ is affine there, and the tests are those of the planar code with
$\phi(x,y)=x$, whose canonical order is $-x$, then the vertical steps, then $+x$.

\begin{lemma}\label{cnt:lem-seam}
For a window of at most $M$ edges, the outcome of every test is the same for all $m>4M^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $m>2M$ the window and its alternatives do not wrap around the torus, and their points have offsets within $M$ of
the offset of the first point of the window. Only (ii) and (iv), and the choice of a geodesic or matching of least
$\Phi$, compare values of $\Phi$, and each comparison is between two edge sets with the same number of edges, at most
$2M$ in total, hence at most $4M$ endpoints. Each side equals $mN+Q$, where $N$ counts its endpoints at negative offsets
and $Q$ is the sum of the offsets of its endpoints. The difference is $m\,\Delta N+\Delta Q$; $\Delta N$ does not
depend on $m$, and $\Delta Q$ does not change if all offsets are shifted by the same amount, so measuring them from the
first point of the window gives $|\Delta Q|\le4M\cdot M<m$. Hence the sign of the difference is the lexicographic sign
of $(\Delta N,\Delta Q)$, which does not depend on $m$.
\end{proof}

The certificates evaluate the tests with a fixed large value in place of $m$, and the Lean statements require
$m\ge L_0=4M(h_0+M)+1$ with $h_0=K+M+1$ (that is, $L_0=1201$ for $M=10$ and $L_0=769$ for $M=8$), which is larger than
$4M^2$.

\subsection{Counting}\label{cnt:count}
For a fixed labelled path, the event that it occurs as $\gamma$ with these labels has probability at most
$p^{n_E}(1-p)^{n_C}$, where $n_E$ and $n_C$ now count the labels of the whole path: its $\mathsf E$-edges lie in $E$,
and its $\mathsf C$-edges lie in $C^\ast$ and therefore not in $E$. Because $n_E\ge n_C$ on $\gamma$, this is at most
$\omega_E^{n_E}\omega_C^{n_C}$ with $\omega_E=pX$ and $\omega_C=(1-p)/X$, for every $X\ge1$.

Fix $M\ge3$ and $K=M-1$. A \emph{state} is a walk of $K$ unit steps through $K+1$ distinct points, with a label on
each step, none of whose windows fails a test. A \emph{successor} of a state appends one labelled step to a point
outside the walk, such that no window of at most $M$ edges that ends with the new step fails a test; the oldest step is
then dropped, and $\omega(s\to s')$ is the weight of the new label. If a positive potential $H$ on the states satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{cnt:row}
  \sum_{s'\ \text{successor of}\ s}\omega(s\to s')\,H(s')\le\lambda\,H(s)\qquad\text{for every state }s,
\end{equation}
then by induction on $n$ the labelled continuations of $n$ steps of a state $s$ weigh at most
$\lambda^{n}H(s)/\min H$. Every labelled simple path none of whose windows of at most $M$ edges fails a test is such a
continuation of its first $K$ steps, because each of its windows of at most $M$ edges ends at some step and lies in the
$M$-step suffix there.

\emph{Planar code.} The tests do not depend on the position of the window, so the states are translation classes of
labelled walks in $\mathbb Z^2$. A state whose points lie in $B$ has no successor inside $B$ that it lacks in
$\mathbb Z^2$, so the rows \eqref{cnt:row} of the system on $\mathbb Z^2$ imply those of all states in $B$, and no
boundary types occur.

\emph{Rotated code.} Here the tests near the top side depend on the heights. Let $h_0=2K+6$. A state whose last point
has height $h<h_0$ carries $h$; it is a \emph{near} state $(s,h)$. A state whose last point has height $h\ge h_0$ is the
translation class $s$ of its walk and gets the potential $H(s)$ of the planar system; the tests of its successors are
those of the planar code, because every point of a window that ends at the new point has height at least
$h_0-1-M=M+3$. The rows to check are therefore: the rows of the near states, whose successors are near states or, at
height $h_0$, far states; the rows of the far states at height exactly $h_0$, whose successors at height $h_0-1$ are
near states; and the rows of the planar system for the far states above $h_0$. Near the bottom side and the rough
sides the walks of $\mathcal R_d$ are among these walks, with the same tests, so these rows suffice for every
$d\ge11$; for small $d$ some modelled heights do not occur.

\emph{Toric code.} Here the tests of windows that straddle the seam depend on the offsets. Let $h_0=K+M+1$. A state
whose last point has offset $o\in[-h_0,h_0-1]$ carries $o$; every other state is the translation class of its walk and
gets the potential of the system on $\mathbb Z^2$ with $\phi(x,y)=x$. Every window of a far state and of its successors
lies on one side of the seam, with offsets at least $h_0-1-M=K$ or at most $-h_0+M=-K-1$, so its tests are those of
the system with $\phi=x$. The rows to check are those of the near states, of the far states at the two interfaces
$o=h_0$ and $o=-h_0-1$, whose successors at offsets $h_0-1$ and $-h_0$ are near states, and of the system with
$\phi=x$. By Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-seam} the tests, and hence the rows, are the same for all $m\ge L_0$. We also need
$m\ge 2h_0+2M+2$, so that the zone near the seam does not wrap around the torus; this is implied by $m\ge L_0$.

\subsection{Proofs}\label{cnt:proofs}
Below, $p_\ast$, $X$ and $\lambda$ are those of Table~\ref{cnt:tab-cert}. For $p<p_\ast$ put
\[
  X_p=X\sqrt{\frac{p_\ast(1-p)}{p(1-p_\ast)}}\ \ge X .
\]
Then $\omega_E$ and $\omega_C$ both scale by $\rho(p_\ast,p)$; the rows are linear in the weights and the tests do not
depend on $p$, so every row holds with $\lambda\rho(p_\ast,p)$ in place of $\lambda$, and the start sums do not
increase. It therefore suffices to prove each bound at $p=p_\ast$, with $\lambda$ replaced by $\lambda\rho(p_\ast,p)$ for
smaller $p$. In each case $\PML\le\PMW$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:planar}]
By Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-rules} and Section~\ref{cnt:regions}, $\PMW(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)$ is at most the total weight of the
labelled paths from $b_1$ to $b_2$ none of whose windows fails a test. Bound the two half-edges of each path, summed
over their labels, by $\omega_E+\omega_C$ each. The interior starts at one of the $w$ checks $(0,y)$ and has at least
$d-2$ edges, so with the certificate and Section~\ref{cnt:count}
\[
  \PMW(\mathcal P_{d,w},p)\le w\,K_0\sum_{\ell\ge d-2}\lambda^{\ell-K}\le w\cdot398.91\cdot\frac{\lambda^{d-11}}{1-\lambda}
\]
with $K=9$ and $\lambda=0.998\,\rho(0.0465,p)$. The condition $d\ge11$ makes the interior at least $K$ steps long, so that
it begins with a state.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rotated}]
As for the planar code, now with the tests and the near states of $\mathcal R_d$, valid for every $d$ by
Section~\ref{cnt:regions}. The path $\gamma$ starts with one of the $d$ half-edges at $b_1$; its interior starts at the
check $z$ of this half-edge, and the first $K$ steps form a state whose first point has height $h(z)$. The interior has
at least $d-2$ edges and ends at a check with at most two half-edges to $b_2$. Hence
\[
  \PMW(\mathcal R_d,p)\le d\cdot2\cdot K_{\mathrm{rot}}\sum_{\ell\ge d-2}\lambda^{\ell-K}\le 2d\cdot366{,}183{,}771.98\cdot\frac{\lambda^{d-11}}{1-\lambda}
\]
with $K=9$ and $\lambda=0.998\,\rho(0.0465,p)$; as for the planar code, $d\ge11$ makes the interior at least $K$ steps long.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:toric}]
Every point of $\mathcal T_m$ can be the first point of $\gamma$. Fix one, and follow $\gamma$ from it. Its first $\ell-1$
steps, where $\ell\ge m$ is the length of $\gamma$, form a labelled simple path whose windows pass the tests, and the
last step closes the cycle; we bound the closing step, summed over its labels, by $\omega_E+\omega_C$, and we do not
test the windows that contain it. The first $K$ steps form a state whose first point has offset $o$. By
Lemma~\ref{cnt:lem-seam} and Section~\ref{cnt:count}, for $m\ge L_0$,
\[
  \PMW(\mathcal T_m,p)\le m^2K_{\mathrm{tor}}\sum_{\ell\ge m}\lambda^{\ell-1-K}=m^2K_{\mathrm{tor}}\frac{\lambda^{m-1-K}}{1-\lambda}.
\]
For $M=10$ we have $K=9$, $h_0=20$, $L_0=1201$, $\lambda=0.997\,\rho(0.046,p)$ and the bound
$m^2\cdot428{,}572{,}391.6\cdot\lambda^{m-10}/(1-\lambda)$. For $M=8$ we have $K=7$, $h_0=16$, $L_0=769$,
$\lambda'=0.996\,\rho(0.045,p)$ and the bound $m^2\cdot383{,}157{,}811\cdot\lambda'^{\,m-8}/(1-\lambda')$.
\end{proof}

\section{Measurement errors}\label{sec:phenom}

In this section we prove Theorem~\ref{thm:phenom}. The decoding graph $G=\mathcal Q_{d,w,T}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:graphs} has the points of the box $B=[0,T]\times[0,w-1]\times[0,d-2]$ as checks, an edge between any
two checks at distance $1$, and half-edges from the checks $(t,x,0)$ to $b_1$ and from the checks $(t,x,d-2)$ to $b_2$.
We write $|u-v|$ for the $\ell^1$ distance of two checks, and a path between checks $u,v$ with $|u-v|$ edges is a
\emph{geodesic}. Every geodesic between two checks of $B$ stays in
$B$, since $B$ is a box.

\subsection{Failure paths and exclusion rules}\label{phen:rules}
Let $\phi(t,x,y)=81t+x+9y$, and for a set $S$ of edges let $\Phi(S)$ be the sum of $\phi(u)+\phi(v)$ over the edges
$\{u,v\}\in S$ between checks. If some correction of least size fails, let $C^\ast$ be one with the least value of $\Phi$ among the failing
corrections of least size.
The set $E\oplus C^\ast$ has even degree at every check and odd degree at $b_1$ and $b_2$, so it contains a simple path
$\gamma$ from $b_1$ to $b_2$. The interior of $\gamma$ (without its two half-edges) meets all $d-1$ rows, so it has at
least $d-2$ edges. Since $C^\ast\oplus\gamma$ is a correction, $|\gamma\cap E|\ge|\gamma\cap C^\ast|$. We label the
edges of $\gamma$ in $E$ by $\mathsf E$ and those in $C^\ast$ by $\mathsf C$; every edge of $\gamma$ gets one label. A
\emph{window} is a sub-path $\sigma$ of $\gamma$ between checks $u\ne v$ that contains no half-edge; $n_E$ and $n_C$
count its labels, and $\sigma_E$, $\sigma_C$ are its labelled edge sets.

\begin{lemma}\label{phen:lem-rules}
Every window $\sigma$ from $u$ to $v$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $n_C-n_E\le|u-v|$;
\item if $n_C-n_E=|u-v|$, then $\Phi(\sigma_C)\le\Phi(\sigma_E)+\Phi(P')$ for every geodesic $P'$ from $u$ to $v$;
\item $n_C\le c(T_\sigma)$, the least total length of a perfect matching of the set $T_\sigma$ of odd-degree checks of
  $\sigma_C$;
\item if $n_C=c(T_\sigma)$, then $\Phi(\sigma_C)\le\sum_k\Phi(P_k)$ for any geodesics $P_k$ joining the pairs of a
  least-length perfect matching of $T_\sigma$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) For a geodesic $P'$ from $u$ to $v$, $C'=C^\ast\oplus\sigma\oplus P'$ is a correction and
$|C'|\le|C^\ast|-n_C+n_E+|u-v|$. (ii) In the equality case $|C'|=|C^\ast|-2k$, where $k$ is the number of edges of
$P'$ in $(C^\ast\setminus\sigma_C)\cup\sigma_E$; hence $k=0$, and $C'$ has least size. It fails, because
$C'\oplus C^\ast=\sigma\oplus P'$ has no half-edge, and $\Phi(C')=\Phi(C^\ast)-\Phi(\sigma_C)+\Phi(\sigma_E)+\Phi(P')$;
the choice of $C^\ast$ gives the inequality. (iii) and (iv) follow in the same way from $C'=C^\ast\oplus\sigma_C\oplus
P''$, where $P''$ is the set of edges used an odd number of times by the $P_k$; if the $P_k$ overlap, then
$|P''|<c(T_\sigma)$ and $|C'|<|C^\ast|$.
\end{proof}

The least value of $\Phi$ over the geodesics from $u$ to $v$ is attained by the geodesic that takes its unit steps in
increasing order of their $\phi$-increments. By (ii), every run of $\mathsf C$-edges of $\gamma$ is this staircase; in
particular two $\mathsf C$-edges that turn a corner turn from the smaller to the larger $\phi$-increment.

\subsection{Counting}\label{phen:count}
The counting of Section~\ref{cnt:count} carries over, with $\mathbb Z^3$ in place of $\mathbb Z^2$ and
Lemma~\ref{phen:lem-rules} in place of the tests. A fixed labelled path occurs as $\gamma$ with probability at most
$\omega_E^{n_E}\omega_C^{n_C}$, with $\omega_E=pX$, $\omega_C=(1-p)/X$ and any $X\ge1$, since $n_E\ge n_C$ on
$\gamma$. States and successors are defined as there, with windows that satisfy the lemma, and if a positive potential
$H$ on the states satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{phen:row}
  \sum_{s'\ \text{successor of}\ s}\omega(s\to s')\,H(s')\le\lambda\,H(s)\qquad\text{for every state }s,
\end{equation}
then the labelled continuations of $m$ steps of a state $s$ weigh at most $\lambda^mH(s)/\min H$; they include every
labelled simple path whose windows satisfy the lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{phen:lem-box}
For a state whose points lie in $B$, every successor inside $B$ is a successor in $\mathbb Z^3$ with the same steps
and labels. Hence the rows \eqref{phen:row} of the translation-invariant system on $\mathbb Z^3$ imply the rows of all
states in $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every alternative used in Lemma~\ref{phen:lem-rules}, a geodesic between two points of the window or a matching
geodesic, lies in the bounding box of the window's points, hence in $B$. So every window excluded in $\mathbb Z^3$ is
excluded in $B$, and in $B$ a state has no successor that it lacks in $\mathbb Z^3$. Since $H>0$, the rows in $B$
follow.
\end{proof}

So one certificate for the bulk system covers every box; no boundary types occur.

\subsection{The certificate}\label{phen:cert}
The certificate is a positive integer potential for the bulk system with $M=8$: $5{,}263{,}174$ states and
$44{,}599{,}560$ transitions, $p_\ast=2/125$, $X=231/50$, $\lambda=499/500$, potentials at most $2^{32}$. Every row
\eqref{phen:row} is checked in exact integer arithmetic. The largest ratio of the two sides of \eqref{phen:row} is
$0.997805$ (the Perron root of the system is $0.995809$), and the start sum
$K_0=(\omega_E+\omega_C)^2\sum_s\omega(s)H(s)/\min H$ is at most $1.2827$. Lean checks the certificate in its kernel,
and also with compiled evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:results}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:phenom}]
By Lemma~\ref{phen:lem-rules} and the counting above, $\PMW(\mathcal Q_{d,w,T},p)$ is at most the total weight of
the labelled paths from $b_1$ to $b_2$ whose windows satisfy the lemma. Bound the two half-edges of each path, summed
over their labels, by $\omega_E+\omega_C$ each. The interior of the path starts at one of the $w(T+1)$ checks of the
rows $y=0$ and has at least $d-2$ edges. With the certificate, Lemma~\ref{phen:lem-box} gives at $p=p_\ast=2/125$
\[
  \PMW(\mathcal Q_{d,w,T},p)\le w(T+1)K_0\sum_{\ell\ge d-2}\lambda^{\ell-7}\le(T+1)\,w\cdot1.2827\cdot
  \frac{\lambda^{d-9}}{1-\lambda}.
\]
The condition $d\ge9$ makes the interior at least $7$ steps long, so that it begins with a state. For $p<p_\ast$ take $X_p=X\sqrt{p_\ast(1-p)/(p(1-p_\ast))}\ge X$. Then $\omega_E$ and $\omega_C$ both scale by
$\rho(p_\ast,p)$; the rows are linear in the weights and the rules do not depend on $p$, so every row holds with
$\lambda\rho(p_\ast,p)$, and $K_0$ does not increase. Finally $\PML\le\PMW$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The certified threshold}\label{sec:lean-ml}

The auxiliary decoder of Section~\ref{sec:cert} is defined on every code size, with block side $L$ and window radius $R$, and $n_x=\max(5,\lceil w/L\rceil)$, $n_y=\lfloor (d-1)/L\rfloor$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:ml}. The probabilities $\varepsilon_t$ that the reference blocks are bad are defined from the first stage of the decoder on a code of $5\times5$ blocks, as in Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp}.

For a first stage with block side $L\ge2$ and window radius $R=L-1$ that satisfies (V6) and (Hcov), Lean derives from the hypothesis $\varepsilon_t\le5\times10^{-4}$ for the five types the bound \eqref{eq:ml} of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} and the limit $0$ of the optimal failure probability for $w=d-1$. The experiment enters through (Bin). \subsection{The sampling-free bounds}\label{sec:lean-count}

The Lean statements of Theorems~\ref{thm:planar}--\ref{thm:phenom} concern the decoding graphs of Section~\ref{sec:graphs}, which are defined in Lean from their checks and edges.
In Lean, $\mathcal P_{d,w}$ is the box $[0,d-1)\times[0,w)$ of checks with half-edges at the two ends of the first coordinate; the Lean statements write $n_x=d-1$ and $n_y=w$, so the exponent $n_x-10$ in Lean is the exponent $d-11$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:planar}.
The phenomenological graph $\mathcal Q_{d,w,T}$ is the box $[0,T+1)\times[0,w)\times[0,d-1)$ with half-edges at the two ends of the last coordinate.
The rotated code is defined by the positions of its $d^2$ qubits and of its $X$ checks, and the logical class of an error is the parity of its flips on the first column; a Lean lemma identifies its failure probability with that of the graph used in the proof.
The toric code $\mathcal T_m$ has the checks $\mathbb Z_m^2$, and a correction fails when $E\oplus C$ crosses one of the two seams of the torus an odd number of times.
In Lean, $\PMW$ is the probability that some correction of least size fails, and $\PML$ is the least failure probability of a map from syndromes to logical classes; a randomized decoder is a mixture of such maps, so it does no better.
Each theorem has a version for $\PMW$ and one for $\PML$; the second follows from the first and $\PML\le\PMW$.
The constants and rates are those of Section~\ref{sec:results}, entered as exact rationals, for example $398.91=39891/100$ and $0.998=499/500$.
The statements have no hypotheses beyond the ranges of $p$ and of the size.

\paragraph{The general argument.}
Each proof consists of a general argument, proved once for each family of graphs, and a finite check of a certificate.
The general argument has four parts: the probability estimates (independence of the edges, the union bound over failure paths, and the change of measure from $p_\ast$ to $p$), a decoder lemma, a counting lemma, and the soundness of the checks.
The decoder lemma states that if some correction of least size fails, then a failing correction of least size with the least value of the tie-breaking potential $\Phi$ exists; $E\oplus C$ then contains a path between the two boundaries (for the toric code, a cycle that is not null-homologous), and no window of this path can be replaced by a witness that passes the check, which covers the exclusion rules of Section~\ref{sec:count} in one statement.
The counting lemma turns the row inequalities of a potential into a bound on the weighted number of legal labelled walks of each length.
The soundness theorems derive the hypotheses of the counting lemma from the Boolean checks of a certificate.
For the rotated code the walk carries its height below the top side while it is near that side, and for the toric code its offset from the seam of $\Phi$ while it is near the seam; a translation lemma shows that the checks of states far from the seam do not depend on the offset, so the far states are checked at a single offset.

\paragraph{Certificates.}
A certificate lists the states of the walk, that is, its last $K$ steps with their labels (for the rotated and the toric code also the height or the offset near the boundary or the seam), gives each state a positive integer potential, and fixes $p_\ast$, $\lambda$ and the constant of the bound.
The check functions verify, for every listed state, that the weighted sum of the potentials of its legal successors is at most $\lambda$ times its own potential and that every successor that is not listed ends in an excluded window; they verify that every legal start of a walk is listed or excluded, and they bound the start sum by the stated constant.
An excluded window counts only if a witness for it passes the witness check. Hence neither the search that finds the witnesses nor the certificate files need to be trusted: a wrong value can only make a check fail.
Tables~\ref{cnt:tab-cert} and~\ref{phen:tab} give the sizes of the certificates.

\paragraph{Compiled evaluation and the kernel.}
A generator turns the certificate files into Lean data modules and writes the sha256 of each input file into the module.
The checks are then evaluated in one of two ways.
With compiled evaluation, the check functions are compiled to machine code and run through \texttt{native\_decide}; each call adds one axiom, which \texttt{\#print axioms} lists, and the compiler and the runtime join the trusted base.
On the kernel route, the certificate is a binary search tree whose nodes hold a state, its potential and the successors that the certificate claims to be excluded; the check functions recurse on this tree, and every check is reduced by the kernel (\texttt{decide +kernel}), which adds no axiom.
The checks are split into pieces of moderate size (ranges of rows or subtrees, and prefixes of the start walks), each piece is a separate theorem, and lemmas proved once combine the pieces.
The planar, the rotated, the phenomenological and the two toric bounds are proved in both ways from the same certificates, with identical statements (Table~\ref{tab:results}).
The Lean sources also contain kernel-checked bounds from two smaller certificates, for the planar code at $p\le0.045$ with $d\ge9$ and for the phenomenological graph at $p\le0.0155$ with $d\ge7$, with $157{,}618$ and $72{,}294$ states.
The data modules can be regenerated from the certificate files, whose sha256 they record; the reproduction manifest lists the input files of each theorem with their hashes.
For the kernel certificates these include the transition or hint files, from which the generator reads the windows that the certificate claims to be excluded.
Section~R.3 of the records supplement gives the cost of the checks behind Table~\ref{tab:results} and lists how many such axioms each compiled-evaluation theorem uses besides the standard ones (Section~\ref{sec:lean-trust}).

\subsection{Scope and trusted base}\label{sec:lean-trust}

Not formalized are the identification of the planar, toric and phenomenological codes with their decoding graphs, which is standard (for the rotated code Lean proves it), the symmetry that carries the planar bounds to the other sector of square codes (Section~\ref{sec:graphs}), the argument of Section~\ref{sec:conf} that the rounding of the samplers' flip probabilities does not change the confidence, and, for Theorems~\ref{thm:ml} and~\ref{thm:d1}, the three conditions under which each holds: they enter the Lean proofs as hypotheses. Some parts are proved on paper only: Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} in its general form, whose full proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}, the sharper constants that follow it and those of Remark~\ref{rem:sharper}, and the single-scale criterion of Section~\ref{sec:disc-stat}, which the criterion supplement proves.

Lean reports, for every theorem, the axioms it depends on. The trusted base is Lean's kernel, including its built-in
arithmetic on natural numbers, together with three standard axioms of Lean's foundations: propositional
extensionality, the axiom of choice and the soundness of quotients. The theorems of Sections~\ref{sec:lean-ml} and~\ref{sec:lean-d1} and the
kernel-route theorems of Section~\ref{sec:lean-count} depend on nothing else. The theorems marked ``compiled
evaluation'' in Table~\ref{tab:results} also depend on one axiom for each large certificate check: Lean evaluates the
check, a Boolean test, with compiled code and records its outcome \texttt{true} as an axiom that names the test.
Beyond the kernel, they rely on Lean's compiler and runtime, on the C compiler that
builds the precompiled modules, and on these modules having been built from the checked sources. Programs outside Lean
generate the certificate data, and the searches for exclusion witnesses are Lean functions; neither needs to be trusted
(Section~\ref{sec:lean-count}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Monotone arguments}\label{sec:disc-mono}
Call an error set \emph{failing} for minimum-weight matching if some minimum-weight correction of it fails. An event is \emph{increasing} if it is preserved when errors are added. The argument of \cite{DKLP02}, the bounds of Sections~\ref{sec:count} and~\ref{sec:phenom}, and every bound obtained by counting paths, clusters or witnesses with a union bound, bound the failure probability by the probability of an increasing event that contains the failing sets. Every such event contains the event $U$ that some subset of the error is failing, and $U$ is itself increasing, so no such bound is smaller than $\Pr(U)$. For the planar code at $p=0.093$, Monte Carlo estimates of $\Pr(U)$, with $U$ decided by integer programming, grow from $0.264$ at $d=7$ to at least $0.357$ at $d=19$, while the failure probability of matching itself does not grow. These estimates are not rigorous, and the behaviour for large $d$ is an extrapolation, but they indicate that arguments of this kind cannot prove a threshold for matching above $0.093$, and in particular that the counting bounds cannot be pushed to the numerical threshold $0.1031$ of matching. The certificates of Theorems~\ref{thm:ml} and~\ref{thm:d1} are not of this kind: a block is bad when window solutions leave a defect or a long residual path, and adding errors can remove both.

Within the counting method, the value of $p$ at which the growth rate of the labelled walks reaches one increases only slowly with the length of the windows that the exclusion rules inspect, from about $0.042$ for windows of four edges to about $0.047$ for windows of eleven edges (Table~\ref{cnt:tab-growth}; floating-point estimates, not part of any proof). We do not know how to go well beyond $0.05$ without sampling. A natural next step would also use the correction away from the crossing, but that is a non-local function of the error.

\subsection{Statistical certification}\label{sec:disc-stat}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} is a theorem about unknown numbers, the five bad-block probabilities, combined with a registered experiment that bounds them. Its confidence statement concerns the sampling only: it bounds the probability that the experiment accepts while the bound is false, under the binomial model, and it does not depend on any prior on the unknown numbers. Anyone can recompute the counts from the archived records, and a new run with fresh seeds would be an independent test of the same statement. The same holds for Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} and its 25 block types, except that its tests of (V6) and (Hcov) at the registered radius ran only after the analysis (Section~\ref{sec:cert-meas}).

A deterministic proof at $p=0.100$ would need the bad-block probabilities themselves, which are probabilities of events depending on about $4\times10^{6}$ independent bits, or a criterion whose inputs can be computed exactly. The criterion supplement (see Code and data) proves such a criterion for optimal decoding, at a single scale. Draw a sample from the posterior law of the errors in a window, given the syndrome in the window. If, for each of four window shapes, in the bulk, at a rough side, at a smooth side and at a corner, the probability that this sample and the true error differ by a long path near the centre of the window, plus twice the square root of a measure of how strongly the errors on the edges that leave the window influence the posterior law near its centre, is less than $1/16$ at $p_\ast$, then the optimal threshold of the planar code is at least $p_\ast$. The inputs need exact posterior samples and partition functions with certified numerics on windows of side near $10^3$. The criterion is not checked in Lean, and we have not certified it at any $p$.

\subsection{Beyond \texorpdfstring{$p=0.100$}{p = 0.100}}
The certified decoder uses exact window posteriors for its weights. With matching weights instead, the bad-block probability at $p=0.10$ stayed at several per cent in pilot runs even for blocks of side $256$ and windows of radius $320$, because the residual loops of matching approach the block size near the matching threshold. Posterior weights remove this obstacle, but the window side needed to make bad blocks rare still grows as $p$ approaches the Nishimori point, and the cost of computing exact window marginals grows like the cube of the window side. We did not attempt a certificate above $p=0.100$.

\subsection{Measurement errors}\label{sec:disc-meas}
Theorem~\ref{thm:d1} carries the method of Theorem~\ref{thm:ml} over to measurement errors; its first stage is plain matching with uniform weights. The value $0.02$ lies below the numerical thresholds for this noise, $0.0293(2)$ for matching \cite{WHP03} and near $0.033$ for optimal decoding \cite{OAIM04}, and above the value $0.016$ that Theorem~\ref{thm:phenom} gives without sampling for every minimum-weight decoder.

\subsection{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item A deterministic proof that the optimal threshold of the surface code exceeds $0.05$, for instance by certifying the single-scale criterion of Section~\ref{sec:disc-stat} with exact arithmetic.
\item A rigorous threshold for minimum-weight matching above $0.0465$. By the discussion above, such a proof cannot rest on an increasing event alone near the numerical threshold of matching.
\item Certified bounds under measurement errors that approach the numerical thresholds, and bounds for circuit-level noise beyond the matching thresholds proved so far.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}}\label{app:proof}

This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, in the explicit form of Theorem~\ref{s1:thm:main}, and records how its constants are evaluated. We use the decoder of Section~\ref{sec:cert-decoder} on $\mathcal P_{H+1,W}$ with $W=n_xL$, $H=n_yL$, $n_x\ge5$ and $n_y\ge4$, and the bad blocks, types, declared rows and hypothesis $(\mathrm H_\eps)$ of Section~\ref{sec:cert-lemma}. For a check $x$, $\beta(x)$ denotes its block; block displacements are measured with the norms $|\cdot|_\infty$ and $|\cdot|_1$ of their indices, $N_k(c)$ is the set of blocks at distance at most $k$ from $c$, and $d(x,y)$ is the least weight of a path between checks $x$ and $y$. Stage~1 is any program as in Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}. Let $F=E\oplus C_0$; its odd checks are $\cD'$.

\subsection{Block events}\label{s1:sec:events}

\begin{lemma}[dependence]\label{s1:lem:dep}
Whether $c$ is bad depends only on the error on the edges that have an endpoint in the union $U(c)$ of the windows of the blocks of $N_1(c)$. With $D=4+\lfloor2R/L\rfloor$, the bad events of any set of blocks with pairwise distances at least $D$ are independent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Whether $c$ is flagged depends on $\cD$ and on $C_0$ on the edges at the checks of $c$; whether $c$ is long depends on $F$ on the edges with both endpoints in $N_1(c)$. Each of these edges has its left or lower endpoint in $N_1(c)$, so it is owned by a block of $N_1(c)$, and its value in $C_0$ is a function of the syndrome in that block's window, which is a function of $E$ on the edges with an endpoint in the window. These edges, and the edges at the checks of $N_1(c)$ themselves, have an endpoint in $U(c)$; so $\bad(c)$ depends only on $E$ on the edges with an endpoint in $U(c)$. In block coordinates, if $c$ is in column $a$, the checks of $U(c)$ have first coordinate in $[aL-L-R,\,aL+2L+R-1]$, and similarly for the rows. For blocks $c,c'$ at distance $n$, say in the first coordinate, an edge with an endpoint in $U(c)$ and an endpoint in $U(c')$, or a check in both, exists only if $aL+2L+R\ge(a+n)L-L-R$, that is, if $(n-3)L\le2R$. Hence the two edge sets are disjoint when $(n-3)L>2R$, which holds for $n\ge4+\lfloor2R/L\rfloor$, and disjoint edge sets carry independent errors.
\end{proof}

If every block of type $t$ is bad with probability $\eps_t$, then $(\mathrm H_\eps)$ holds with $\eps=\max_t\eps_t$, by Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:dep}.

\subsection{The deterministic lemma}\label{s1:sec:det}
From now on, $a_0\ge\max(2,D-1)$, $a_1\ge a_0$, $a_1\ge2D-2$, and $a_1$ is even. (The condition $a_1\ge a_0$ is used only in the strip argument below.) The lemmas of this section carry the letters (A), (B), (C), (W) and (X) of the outline of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}.

\paragraph{Clusters.} With the blocks of the declared rows counted as bad, the \emph{clusters} are the components of the bad blocks (declared or not) under adjacency at distance at most $a_0$. Distinct clusters are at distance at least $a_0+1\ge D$. $\Gbot$ and $\Gtop$ are the clusters that contain the declared rows at the bottom and at the top (possibly the same cluster). Every other cluster is \emph{interior}; its blocks lie in the rows $2+a_0,\dots,n_y-3-a_0$.

\begin{lemma}[A]\label{s1:lem:A}
Take the components of $F$ on the checks (a half-edge belongs to the component of its check; $b_1$ and $b_2$ join nothing). A component of $F$ that meets no bad block has no odd check and lies within distance $L-1$ of each block it meets. Every component of $F$ meets at most one cluster.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $c$ be a good block. Then $c$ is in the rows $2,\dots,n_y-3$, and no path of $F$ from a check of $c$ reaches $\ell_\infty$ distance $L$ from $c$: every edge changes each coordinate by at most $1$, so the first check of such a path at distance at least $L$ from $c$ is at distance exactly $L$, and the path up to that check lies in $N_1(c)$, which would make $c$ long. (Since $c$ is in rows $2,\dots,n_y-3$, the path meets no half-edge before that check.) The odd checks of $F$ are the residual defects, which lie in flagged, hence bad, blocks; so a component meeting no bad block has none.

Suppose a component meets two clusters $K_1\ne K_2$. A path in it from $K_1$ to $K_2$ leaves $K_1$ into a block $c'$ adjacent to $K_1$, which is good because $a_0\ge1$. The whole component then lies within distance $L-1$ of $c'$, so it meets only blocks within distance $1$ of $c'$; in particular $K_2$ has a block within distance $1$ of $c'$, hence within distance $2\le a_0$ of $K_1$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Since flagged blocks are bad and the level-0 adjacency equals the cluster adjacency, every level-0 component lies in one cluster. Two clusters are \emph{linked} if a level-1 component (resolved or not) meets both, and the \emph{families} are the classes of clusters under the equivalence generated by linking. For a family $\cF$ let $Y_\cF$ be the sum of the components of $F$ that meet $\cF$ and of the corrections $C_K$ of the resolved components inside $\cF$.

\begin{lemma}[B, decomposition]\label{s1:lem:B}
$\partial Y_\cF=\Omega\cap\cF$ for every family, and
\[
E\oplus C=\Bigl(\sum_{\text{components of $F$ meeting no bad block}}\Bigr)\oplus\bigoplus_\cF Y_\cF\oplus\Ctop .
\]
The components of $F$ that meet no bad block are closed and do not change the class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$E\oplus C=F\oplus\bigoplus_KC_K\oplus\Ctop$, and $F$ is the sum of its components; by Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:A}, each component meeting a bad block meets exactly one cluster, hence one family. The components of $F$ meeting $\cF$ have as odd checks the residual defects in the flagged blocks of $\cF$, and each resolved $C_K$ inside $\cF$ has odd checks $\cD'\cap K$. Every flagged block of $\cF$ is resolved exactly once, or its residual belongs to $\Omega$; this gives $\partial Y_\cF=\Omega\cap\cF$. A component meeting no bad block has no odd check and lies within distance $L-1$ of a good block, so it touches no half-edge and its height is less than $3L\le H$; it is therefore a cycle of checks with no half-edge, which does not change the parity at $b_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[C, failure path]\label{s1:lem:C}
If the decoder fails, then one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(T\arabic*),leftmargin=2.5em]
\item some family $\cF$ has $Y_\cF$ with odd degree at both $b_1$ and $b_2$;
\item there is a sequence $b_1\to\cF_1\to\dots\to\cF_m\to b_2$ of distinct families in which consecutive families are joined by paths of $\Ctop$, and the first (last) link is a path of $\Ctop$ from $b_1$ (to $b_2$) or an odd degree of $Y_{\cF_1}$ at $b_1$ (of $Y_{\cF_m}$ at $b_2$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Decompose $\Ctop$ into paths that pair the checks of $\Omega$ with each other or with a boundary vertex, and form the multigraph whose vertices are the families and $b_1,b_2$: it has an edge for each such path (between the families, or the family and boundary vertex, containing its ends) and an edge $\cF b_i$ whenever $Y_\cF$ has odd degree at $b_i$. By the handshake lemma for $Y_\cF$ and Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:B}, every family vertex has even degree. If the decoder fails, $E\oplus C$ has odd degree at $b_1$, and by Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:B} so has $b_1$ in the multigraph; hence $b_1$ and $b_2$ lie in one component of the multigraph, and a simple path joins them. If this path is $b_1\to\cF\to b_2$ and both of its edges are odd degrees of $Y_\cF$, this is case (T1); otherwise the path is as in (T2). (A minimum-weight $\Ctop$ contains no path between two boundary vertices, since weights are positive.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[W, parity witnesses]\label{s1:lem:W}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(W\arabic*),leftmargin=2.8em,start=0]
\item An odd level-0 component $K$ in an interior cluster has, in the same cluster, a flagged block at distance at least $a_0+1$ from $K$.
\item Let $K_1$ be an odd level-1 component such that every cluster meeting $K_1$ is interior. Then one of these clusters contains a flagged block of another level-1 component, at distance at least $a_1+1$ from $K_1$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R),leftmargin=2.8em]
\item Every check of $\Omega$ lies in a flagged block of an odd level-0 component whose blocks belong to an odd level-1 component. Consequently, a path of $\Ctop$ between two different families of a (T2) chain joins blocks at distance at least $a_1+1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The components of $F$ that meet an interior cluster reach no rough side, since the cluster's blocks lie in the rows $2+a_0,\dots,n_y-3-a_0$ and the components stay within distance $L-1$ of good blocks outside it. Their odd checks are the residual defects in the cluster's flagged blocks, so the number of these defects is even. For (W0): $|\cD'\cap K|$ is odd, so the cluster has residual defects outside $K$, that is, in another level-0 component, which is at distance at least $a_0+1$ from $K$. For (W1), add to the components of $F$ meeting an interior cluster the resolved level-0 corrections inside it; the odd checks of the sum are the residual defects of the cluster's odd level-0 components, again an even number. Summing over the clusters that meet $K_1$, all interior by assumption, the total number of such defects is even, while $K_1$ contains an odd number of them; so some cluster has level-1 residual outside $K_1$, that is, in a flagged block of another level-1 component, at distance at least $a_1+1$ from $K_1$. (R) holds by the construction of $\Omega$. Blocks of distinct level-1 components are more than $a_1$ apart, and the two ends of a path of $\Ctop$ between different families lie in different level-1 components, because a level-1 component lies in a single family.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Strips and the sub-path of a chain.} Put $h_s=a_1/2+1$. The bottom strip $\Sbot$ is the set of block rows $\le h_s$, the top strip $\Stop$ the set of rows $\ge n_y-1-h_s$. A \emph{bottom (top) strip family} is a family with a block in $\Sbot$ ($\Stop$); a family containing $\Gbot$ is a bottom strip family. A family $\cF$ with $Y_\cF$ of odd degree at $b_1$ is also a bottom strip family: a half-edge of $Y_\cF$ at $b_1$ lies in a component of $F$ meeting a bad block of row $0$ or $1$, which then belongs to $\Gbot$, or in a correction $C_K$ whose region reaches row $0$, so that $K$ has a block within $\max(\lceil a_0/2\rceil,a_1/2)\le h_s$ rows of row $0$. The same holds at the top. On a (T2) chain, let $\cF_0$ be the last bottom strip family ($b_1$ if there is none) and $\cF_{m+1}$ the first top strip family after it ($b_2$ if there is none); the families $\cF_1,\dots,\cF_m$ between them are \emph{intermediate}. If $\cF_0$ is also a top strip family, it has blocks in both strips; this case is counted with (T1) in Section~\ref{s1:sec:bound}, so we assume $\cF_0\ne\cF_{m+1}$ there. An intermediate family contains no declared block and no block of the strips; its level-1 regions $N_{a_1/2}(K)$ stay in the rows $\ge2$ and its clusters are interior, so (W0) and (W1) apply to all of them. Residual defects lie in unresolved components, whose regions miss the rough rows; hence the entry and exit defects of every family on the chain lie in the rows $3,\dots,n_y-4$, never in a declared row.

For $1\le i\le m$ let $u_i$ and $v_i$ be the checks of $\Omega$ at which the chain enters and leaves $\cF_i$, let $v_0$ be the check at which it leaves $\cF_0$ and $u_{m+1}$ the check at which it enters $\cF_{m+1}$, and let $P_i$ be the path of $\Ctop$ from $v_i$ to $u_{i+1}$.

\begin{lemma}[X, exchange]\label{s1:lem:X}
$\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^m w(P_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^md(u_i,v_i)+d(v_0,b_1)+d(u_{m+1},b_2)$, where $d(x,b_j)$ is the least weight of a path from $x$ to a half-edge at $b_j$, the half-edge included.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Remove $P_0,\dots,P_m$ from $\Ctop$, join $u_i$ to $v_i$ by a shortest path for $1\le i\le m$, and join $v_0$ to $b_1$ and $u_{m+1}$ to $b_2$ by shortest paths. The result is again an edge set whose odd checks are $\Omega$, and $\Ctop$ has minimum weight among such sets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The probability bound}\label{s1:sec:bound}
\subsubsection{Metric facts}\label{s1:sec:metric}
Write $|z|_1$ for the $\ell_1$ norm. Since every edge changes $|\cdot|_1$ by at most $1$ and weighs at least $0.98$, every path between checks $x$ and $y$ weighs at least $0.98\,|x-y|_1$. A monotone lattice path inside the rectangle of checks gives $d(x,y)\le1.02\,|x-y|_1$, and a straight path down to row $0$ followed by a half-edge gives $d(x,b_1)\le1.02\,(y_x+1)$ for a check $x$ in row $y_x$, and similarly at the top. For checks $x,y$ in blocks $c,c'$,
\[
L\,(|c'-c|_1-\nu)\le|x-y|_1\le L\,(|c'-c|_1+\nu),\qquad\nu=2,
\]
so the offset between a check and its block costs at most $\nu$ in units of $L$. Put $\kappa=1.05\ge1.02/0.98$.

\subsubsection{Selection trees}\label{s1:sec:select}
For a cluster $\mathcal C$ and a block of it chosen as its \emph{root}, process the blocks of $\mathcal C$ in breadth-first order for the adjacency at distance at most $a_0$, starting from the root, and \emph{select} a block if it is at distance at least $D$ from every block selected before.

\begin{lemma}\label{s1:lem:select}
(i) The selected blocks are pairwise at distance at least $D$. (ii) Every block of $\mathcal C$ is within distance $D-1$ of a selected block. (iii) Every selected block other than the root has a selected block processed before it at a distance in $[D,A_0]$, $A_0=D-1+a_0$; so the selected blocks form a tree $\tau_{\mathcal C}$ whose steps lie in $S_0=\{v\in\mathbb Z^2:D\le|v|_\infty\le A_0\}$. Selected blocks of distinct clusters are at distance at least $a_0+1\ge D$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) and (ii) hold by construction. For (iii), a block $b$ other than the root is $a_0$-adjacent to a block $b''$ processed before it, and $b''$ is within $D-1$ of a block $b'$ selected before $b$; then $|b-b'|_\infty\le a_0+D-1=A_0$, and $|b-b'|_\infty\ge D$ because $b$ was selected. The last claim holds because distinct clusters are at distance at least $a_0+1$.
\end{proof}

Hence every set of selected blocks, of one or several clusters, that avoids the declared rows satisfies the hypothesis of $(\mathrm H_\eps)$.

\subsubsection{Generating functions}\label{s1:sec:gf}
Put $A_1=D-1+a_1$, $S_1=\{v\in\mathbb Z^2:D\le|v|_\infty\le A_1\}$, $T_0=|S_0|$, $T_1=|S_1|$ and $\sigma_w=a_1+2-D$. For $\lambda>0$ and $s\ge0$ let
\[
t(v)=e^{\lambda\kappa|v|_1+s\,v_y},\qquad \Tt_0=\sum_{v\in S_0}t(v),\qquad \Tt_1=\sum_{v\in S_1}t(v),
\]
where $v_y$ is the signed row component of $v$. Define
\begin{gather*}
U_0=\eps T_0,\quad \Ut=\eps\Tt_0,\quad \tilde Q=\eps T_0+\frac{\Ut}{1-\Ut},\quad \tilde V=\eps\Tt_1\tilde Q,\quad V_0=\eps T_1\Bigl(\eps T_0+\frac{U_0}{1-U_0}\Bigr),\\
W_0=\frac1{1-\Ut},\qquad B_0=\frac1{1-U_0},\qquad Q_m=\bigl((1-\Ut)^{-2}-1\bigr)B_0+\eps T_0+B_0-1 .
\end{gather*}
For $a,c\ge0$ let $M_{a,c}=\sum_{x:\,|x|_\infty\ge\sigma_w}\bigl(w^{*a}*\mathbf 1_{S_0}^{*c}\bigr)(x)$, where $w=\mathbf 1_{S_0}\,t$ and $*$ is convolution on $\mathbb Z^2$: it counts tree paths with $a$ tilted and then $c$ untilted steps in $S_0$ that end at $\ell_\infty$ distance at least $\sigma_w$. Put
\[
\mathrm{Wit}_{\mathrm{on}}=W_0\sum_{a,c\ge0}\eps^{a+c}M_{a,c},\qquad \mathrm{Wit}_{\mathrm{off}}=\sum_{c\ge1}\eps^cM_{0,c}\big|_{\lambda=s=0},
\]
$\mathrm{off}=(1+\kappa)D\nu$, and
\[
\Phi=e^{\lambda\,\mathrm{off}}\,\eps\,\Bigl[\mathrm{Wit}_{\mathrm{on}}+\frac{Q_m\,\eps T_1\,\mathrm{Wit}_{\mathrm{off}}}{1-V_0}\Bigr](1-\tilde V)^{-2},\qquad
J=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb Z^2,\,|\Delta|_\infty\ge\sigma_w}e^{-\lambda|\Delta|_1+s\Delta_y},\qquad r=J\,\Phi .
\]
$J$ is finite if and only if $s<\lambda$. For the spanning case let $T_1(s)=\sum_{v\in S_1}e^{sv_y}$, $U_0(s)=\eps\sum_{v\in S_0}e^{sv_y}$ and
\[
V_1(s)=\eps T_1(s)\Bigl(\eps T_0+\frac{U_0(s)}{1-U_0(s)}\Bigr).
\]

\subsubsection{The theorem}
\begin{theorem}\label{s1:thm:main}
Let $L\ge2$, $0\le R<L$, $D=4+\lfloor2R/L\rfloor$, $a_0\ge\max(2,D-1)$, and let $a_1$ be even with $a_1\ge a_0$ and $a_1\ge2D-2$; put $h_s=a_1/2+1$ and $H_0=2h_s+A_1+1=2a_1+D+2$. Assume $(\mathrm H_\eps)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item If $V_1(0)<0.9$, then with $s_1=\sup\{s\ge0:V_1(s)\le0.9,\ U_0(s)\le0.9\}$,
\[
\Pr(\mathrm{T1},\text{ or (T2) with }\cF_0=\cF_{m+1})\le C_1\,n_x\,e^{-s_1(n_y-H_0)},\qquad C_1=\frac{(h_s+1)\,W_1\,(1+\eps T_1(s_1)W_1)}{1-V_1(s_1)},
\]
where $W_1=1/(1-U_0(s_1))$.
\item If at some $0\le s<\lambda$ we have $\Ut<1$, $\tilde V<1$, $V_0<1$ and $r<1$, then
\[
\Pr(\mathrm{T2},\ \cF_0\ne\cF_{m+1})\le C_2\,n_x\,e^{-s(n_y-H_0)},\qquad C_2=\frac{4(h_s+1)\,G_{\mathrm{bot}}\,J\,E_{\mathrm{top}}\,e^{\lambda c_{\mathrm{ends}}}}{1-r},
\]
where $G_{\mathrm{bot}}=W_0/(1-\tilde V)$, $E_{\mathrm{top}}=\eps W_0(1+\eps\Tt_1W_0)/(1-\tilde V)$ and $c_{\mathrm{ends}}=D\nu+\kappa D\nu+\kappa\nu+\kappa(2h_s+A_1)+2.04\,\kappa/L$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the failure probability of the decoder on $\mathcal P_{n_yL+1,n_xL}$ is at most $C_1n_xe^{-s_1(n_y-H_0)}+C_2n_xe^{-s_2(n_y-H_0)}$ with $s_2=s$.
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} with explicit constants; the constants depend on $L$ only through the last term of $c_{\mathrm{ends}}$, which decreases in $L$. The rest of this section proves it. Throughout, a \emph{configuration} is an error $E$, and every sum over block tuples counts each tuple once; tuples that cannot arise only add nonnegative terms.

\subsubsection{Routes through a family}\label{s1:sec:routes}
Fix a family $\cF$ of a (T2) chain, with entry check $u$ and exit check $v$ (for $\cF_0$ only an exit, for $\cF_{m+1}$ only an entry). Join two clusters of $\cF$ if some level-1 component has odd level-0 components $o$ in one and $o'$ in the other at distance at most $a_1$. This graph on the clusters of $\cF$ is connected, because each level-1 component is $a_1$-connected and each odd level-0 component lies in one cluster. Take a simple path $c_0=\mathcal C(u),\dots,c_t=\mathcal C(v)$ in it, where $\mathcal C(x)$ is the cluster containing $\beta(x)$, and for each step a pair $(o,o')$ at distance at most $a_1$ with blocks $b_o\in o$ and $b_{o'}\in o'$ at distance at most $a_1$. Root $c_0$ at $\beta(u)$ and $c_{j+1}$ at $b_{o'}$, and select blocks as in Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:select}. The \emph{route} of $\cF$ is the sequence of selected blocks formed by the tree path in $c_j$ from its root to the selected block $s_j$ that covers $b_o$ (in-cluster steps in $S_0$), the \emph{link} $s_j\to\mathrm{root}(c_{j+1})$, and so on, ending in $c_t$ with the tree path from its root to the selected block $\sigma(\cF)$ that covers $\beta(v)$. A link has length at least $a_0+1\ge D$ (the clusters are distinct) and at most $(D-1)+a_1=A_1$, so its step lies in $S_1$.

\emph{Leaves.} Every root is a block of an odd level-0 component: $\beta(u)$ by (R), and $b_{o'}$ because $o'$ belongs to a level-1 component. In an interior cluster, by (W0) and Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:select}(ii) the cluster has at least two selected blocks. For every route cluster whose route part is its root alone, other than the cluster $\mathcal C^*$ below, add one tree neighbour of the root (a \emph{leaf}, with displacement in $S_0$, not on the route).

\emph{Witness.} Let $K_j$ and $K'_j$ be the level-1 components of the links that enter and leave $c_j$, with $K_0=K(u)$ and $K'_t=K(v)$, the level-1 components of $u$ and $v$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[($\alpha$)] If $K_j\ne K'_j$ for some $j$, put $\mathcal C^*=c_j$ and $e^*=\mathrm{root}(c_j)$, a block of $K_j$. The block $b_o$ of the leaving link (or $\beta(v)$ if $j=t$) belongs to $K'_j$, so it is at distance at least $a_1+1$ from $e^*$, and the selected block $b'$ that covers it satisfies $|b'-e^*|_\infty\ge a_1+1-(D-1)=\sigma_w$.
\item[($\alpha'$)] Otherwise every link belongs to $K=K(u)=K(v)$, which is odd because it carries the residual check $u$, and by (W1) a cluster $\mathcal C^*$ meeting $K$ contains a block of a level-1 component $K'\ne K$. If $\mathcal C^*$ is a route cluster, its root $e^*$ is a block of $K$, and the selected block $b'$ covering the block of $K'$ satisfies $|b'-e^*|_\infty\ge\sigma_w$.
\item[($\beta$)] If $\mathcal C^*$ is not a route cluster, follow a chain of odd level-0 components of $K$ from a route cluster $\mathcal C_x$ to $\mathcal C^*$, shortened so that the clusters after $\mathcal C_x$ are distinct and off the route, and root each of them at the block reached by the link into it (an \emph{excursion}); again $e^*=\mathrm{root}(\mathcal C^*)$ is a block of $K$ and $|b'-e^*|_\infty\ge\sigma_w$.
\end{itemize}
In all cases the tree path $\pi$ in $\mathcal C^*$ from $e^*$ to $b'$ has at least one step, so $\mathcal C^*$ needs no leaf. The \emph{skeleton} of $\cF$ consists of the route, the leaves, $\pi$ and, in case ($\beta$), the excursion: the tree path in $\mathcal C_x$ from a route block to the start of the first excursion link, the excursion links, and the excursion clusters with their own leaves. All skeleton blocks are distinct selected blocks, so by Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:select} and $(\mathrm H_\eps)$ the probability that all of them are bad is at most $\eps$ to the number of them. (The skeleton of an intermediate family contains no declared block.)

\subsubsection{Proof of part (a)}
Let $\cF$ be a family with blocks in both strips; this covers (T1), where some $Y_\cF$ has odd degree at $b_1$ and at $b_2$, and the case $\cF_0=\cF_{m+1}$. Take, as in Section~\ref{s1:sec:routes}, a route through the clusters of $\cF$ from a block in $\Sbot$ or in a declared bottom row to a block in $\Stop$ or in a declared top row, with leaves on the link-entered clusters. If $\cF$ contains $\Gbot$, choose the route to start at a declared block of $\Gbot$; this is possible because the graph on the clusters of $\cF$ is connected, and then the route, a simple path in that graph, never returns to $\Gbot$. Cut it at the last block $b_s$ that lies in $\Sbot$ or in a declared row and at the first later block that lies in $\Stop$ or in a declared row; the counted part runs from $b_s$ to the block $b_e$ before that cut. There are at most $(h_s+1)\,n_x$ choices for $b_s$, which is not counted with a factor $\eps$; every later route block and every leaf is bad, selected and not declared. Every later link enters an interior cluster, which has (W0), except possibly one link into the non-declared part of $\Gtop$, whose target may lack (W0). The block $b_e$ is within $A_1$ rows of a block of $\Stop$ or of a declared row, so the signed row progress from $b_s$ to $b_e$ is at least $(n_y-1-h_s-A_1)-h_s=n_y-H_0$. Multiplying the indicator by $e^{s(\text{progress}-(n_y-H_0))}\ge1$ and distributing $e^{s\,\text{progress}}$ over the steps gives, summed over all counted routes,
\[
(h_s+1)\,n_x\cdot W_1\cdot\sum_{k\ge0}V_1(s)^k\cdot(1+\eps T_1(s)W_1)\cdot e^{-s(n_y-H_0)},
\]
since the in-cluster steps from $b_s$ contribute $\sum_kU_0(s)^k=W_1$, each link into an interior cluster contributes $\eps T_1(s)$ times $\eps T_0$ (root with a leaf, the route staying at the root) plus $U_0(s)/(1-U_0(s))$ (one or more route steps), which is $V_1(s)$, and the one exceptional link contributes at most $1+\eps T_1(s)W_1$. At $s=s_1$ this is $C_1n_xe^{-s_1(n_y-H_0)}$.

\subsubsection{The counting lemma for intermediate families}
\begin{lemma}\label{s1:lem:T2}
Let $\cF_1,\dots,\cF_m$ be the intermediate families of a (T2) chain, $D'_i=\mathrm{root}(\cF_{i+1})-\sigma(\cF_i)$ the jump displacements ($0\le i\le m$, with $\sigma(\cF_0)$ the block of $\cF_0$'s route that covers $\beta(v_0)$) and $R_i=\sigma(\cF_i)-\mathrm{root}(\cF_i)$ the route displacements ($1\le i\le m$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item $|D'_i|_\infty\ge\sigma_w$ for every jump;
\item $\sum_{i=0}^m|D'_i|_1\le\kappa\sum_{i=1}^m|R_i|_1+m\,\mathrm{off}+c_{\mathrm{ends}}+\kappa\,\rho_{\mathrm{bot}}+\kappa\,\rho_{\mathrm{top}}$, where $\rho_{\mathrm{bot}}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{top}}$ are the $\ell_1$ lengths, in blocks, of the end routes of Section~\ref{s1:sec:ends};
\item summed over all skeletons of $\cF_1,\dots,\cF_m$ and all landing roots, with the weight $\eps$ per skeleton block, $e^{\lambda\kappa|v|_1+sv_y}$ per route step $v$, and $e^{-\lambda|D'|_1+sD'_y}$ per jump, each intermediate family contributes at most $\Phi$ and each jump at most $J$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) By (R), $v_i$ and $u_{i+1}$ lie in odd level-0 components of odd level-1 components, in $\cF_i$ and $\cF_{i+1}$; a level-1 component lies in one family, so these level-1 components differ, and blocks of different level-1 components are at distance at least $a_1+1$. Since $|\sigma(\cF_i)-\beta(v_i)|_\infty\le D-1$, we get $|D'_i|_\infty\ge a_1+1-(D-1)=\sigma_w$.

(ii) By Section~\ref{s1:sec:metric}, the path $P_i$ weighs at least $0.98\,L\,(|D'_i|_1-D\nu)$: the root of $\cF_{i+1}$ is $\beta(u_{i+1})$, and $\sigma(\cF_i)$ is within $D-1$ of $\beta(v_i)$, which costs $(D-1)\nu$, plus $\nu$ for the offsets of the checks in their blocks. Likewise $d(u_i,v_i)\le1.02\,L\,(|R_i|_1+D\nu)$. Inserting these and the end bounds of Section~\ref{s1:sec:ends} into Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:X} and dividing by $0.98\,L$ gives (ii), since $1.02/0.98\le\kappa$ and $\mathrm{off}=(1+\kappa)D\nu$.

(iii) For every configuration in (T2) choose one skeleton for each intermediate family as in Section~\ref{s1:sec:routes}. On that configuration (ii) holds for the data of the chosen skeletons, so
\[
1\le\exp\Bigl(\lambda\Bigl(\kappa\sum_i|R_i|_1+m\,\mathrm{off}+c_{\mathrm{ends}}+\kappa\rho_{\mathrm{bot}}+\kappa\rho_{\mathrm{top}}-\sum_i|D'_i|_1\Bigr)\Bigr),
\]
and $|R_i|_1$ is at most the sum of the $\ell_1$ norms of the route steps of $\cF_i$. The signed row progress of the block path from $b_s$ to $b_e$ (Section~\ref{s1:sec:ends}) is the sum of the row components of all route steps and jumps, in-block offsets telescoping, and it is at least $n_y-H_0$; so also $1\le e^{s(\text{progress}-(n_y-H_0))}$. Taking expectations and the union over skeletons, the probability is at most the sum, over all skeleton tuples, of the product of these factors and $\eps$ per skeleton block. The sum factorizes. Per jump, the landing root of $\cF_{i+1}$ ranges over the $\Delta=D'_i$ with $|\Delta|_\infty\ge\sigma_w$: factor $J$. Per family in cases ($\alpha$), ($\alpha'$): the route clusters $c_0,\dots,c_t$, one of which is $\mathcal C^*$. Each link contributes $\eps\Tt_1$ (its target block and its tilted step), and each route cluster other than $\mathcal C^*$ contributes $\tilde Q$: either the root with a leaf ($\eps T_0$, the route staying at the root) or $k\ge1$ tilted route steps ($\Ut^k$). $\mathcal C^*$ contributes $\mathrm{Wit}_{\mathrm{on}}$: its route part from $e^*$ shares an initial segment of $a$ tilted steps with $\pi$, $\pi$ continues with $c$ untilted steps and ends at distance at least $\sigma_w$ from $e^*$ (the count $M_{a,c}$), and the rest of the route part from the branching block contributes $W_0$. The position of $\mathcal C^*$ among the $t+1$ route clusters gives $\sum_t(t+1)\tilde V^t=(1-\tilde V)^{-2}$, and the landing root gives $\eps$. In case ($\beta$), the marked route cluster $\mathcal C_x$ contributes $Q_m$: a route part with $k\ge1$ steps, $k+1$ choices of the branching block and the excursion's in-cluster walk ($B_0$), or the root with a leaf and an empty excursion walk ($\eps T_0$), or a non-empty excursion walk from the root ($B_0-1$); the excursion links are untilted ($\eps T_1$ each), the excursion clusters contribute $\sum_jV_0^j=(1-V_0)^{-1}$, and $\mathcal C^*$ contributes $\mathrm{Wit}_{\mathrm{off}}$ through $\pi$ from its root. Together with $e^{\lambda\,\mathrm{off}}$ per family this gives at most $\Phi$ per intermediate family.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The ends, and proof of part (b)}\label{s1:sec:ends}
Assume $\cF_0\ne\cF_{m+1}$, and first that both are strip families.

\emph{Bottom end.} Choose a route in $\cF_0$ that ends at the selected block $\sigma(\cF_0)$ covering $\beta(v_0)$ and that starts in a declared row of $\Gbot$, if $\cF_0$ contains $\Gbot$ (the tree path of $\Gbot$ from a declared block, then links into interior clusters; the route never returns to $\Gbot$), and otherwise at a block of $\Sbot$. Let $b_s$ be the last block of the route that is declared or lies in $\Sbot$. There are at most $(h_s+1)\,n_x$ positions for $b_s$, each with weight at most $1$; every later route block has its factor $\eps$, and every later link enters an interior cluster, which has (W0). The route from $b_s$ therefore contributes at most $G_{\mathrm{bot}}=W_0/(1-\tilde V)$. Its length $\rho_{\mathrm{bot}}=|\sigma(\cF_0)-b_s|_1$ enters (ii) above through
\[
d(v_0,b_1)\le1.02\,L\,\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{bot}}+D\nu\bigr)+1.02\,\bigl(h_sL+1.02\bigr),
\]
a path from $v_0$ to a check of $b_s$, then straight down at most $h_sL$ rows, then a half-edge of weight at most $1.02$. Divided by $0.98\,L$, with $1.02/0.98\le\kappa$, the last term gives $\kappa h_s+1.02\,\kappa/L$; the two ends together give the term $2.04\,\kappa/L$ of $c_{\mathrm{ends}}$.

\emph{Top end.} The route of $\cF_{m+1}$ starts at the landing root $\beta(u_{m+1})$, which has its factor $\eps$ (it is never declared), and stops before the first block that is declared or lies in $\Stop$; let $b_e$ be its last block. It may enter the non-declared part of $\Gtop$ once by a link whose target lacks (W0), with weight $\eps\Tt_1$ instead of $\tilde V$, and then continues by in-cluster steps; so it contributes at most $E_{\mathrm{top}}=\eps W_0(1+\eps\Tt_1W_0)/(1-\tilde V)$. Both of its ends are selected blocks, and $b_e$ lies within $h_s+A_1$ block rows of the top row, so with $\rho_{\mathrm{top}}=|b_e-\beta(u_{m+1})|_1$,
\[
d(u_{m+1},b_2)\le1.02\,L\,\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{top}}+\nu\bigr)+1.02\,\bigl((h_s+A_1)L+1.02\bigr).
\]

\emph{Assembly.} The chain from $\cF_0$ to $\cF_{m+1}$ has $m$ intermediate families and $m+1$ jumps; the extra jump adds $D\nu$ in (ii). Adding the two end bounds, divided by $0.98\,L$, gives the constant $c_{\mathrm{ends}}=D\nu+\kappa D\nu+\kappa\nu+\kappa(2h_s+A_1)+2.04\,\kappa/L$ of the theorem. The signed row progress from $b_s$ (row at most $h_s$) to $b_e$ (row at least $n_y-1-h_s-A_1$) is at least $n_y-H_0$. By Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:T2}(iii), the probability of a (T2) chain with strip families at both ends is at most
\[
(h_s+1)\,n_x\cdot G_{\mathrm{bot}}\cdot J\sum_{m\ge0}r^m\cdot E_{\mathrm{top}}\cdot e^{\lambda c_{\mathrm{ends}}}\cdot e^{-s(n_y-H_0)} .
\]
If $\cF_0=b_1$ or $\cF_{m+1}=b_2$, the chain starts or ends with a path of $\Ctop$ to the boundary. The landing sum of such a boundary match over the $n_x$ block columns is dominated by the corresponding strip factor $(h_s+1)\,n_x\,G_{\mathrm{bot}}\,J$, respectively $J\,E_{\mathrm{top}}$, and a factor $4$ covers the four variants. With $\sum_mr^m=(1-r)^{-1}$ this is $C_2\,n_x\,e^{-s(n_y-H_0)}$. This proves part (b), and with part (a) and Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:C} the theorem.

\subsection{Evaluation of the constants}\label{s1:sec:numbers}
\paragraph{Method.} All counts are exact integers: $T_0$, $T_1$ and the sums over $S_0$ and $S_1$ are finite sums. The witness counts $M_{a,c}$ for $a+c\le9$ are computed by discrete Fourier convolution on a grid large enough that no wrap-around occurs, which reproduces the direct convolution up to rounding; the remaining terms with $a+c\ge10$ are bounded by the sum over all walks, $\sum_{n\ge10}\sum_{a=0}^n\Ut^{\,a}U_0^{\,n-a}$. The landing sum has the closed form
\[
J=\frac{1+x}{1-x}\Bigl(1+\frac{y_+}{1-y_+}+\frac{y_-}{1-y_-}\Bigr)-\sum_{|\Delta|_\infty<\sigma_w}e^{-\lambda|\Delta|_1+s\Delta_y},\qquad x=e^{-\lambda},\ y_\pm=e^{-(\lambda\mp s)},
\]
valid for $s<\lambda$. In the numerical evaluation the offset is taken as $\mathrm{off}+\kappa/2$, which only increases $r$. For given $\eps$, the rate $s$ of part (b) is the largest $s$ (by bisection) for which $\min_\lambda r(\lambda,s)\le0.9$ over a geometric grid of $\lambda$, possibly multiplied by a fixed fraction in $\{1,0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6\}$ chosen to minimize the code size at which the bound drops below $0.1$; then $\lambda$ is the minimizer at that $s$. The rate $s_1$ of part (a) is found by bisection. All arithmetic is in double precision. The stated constants are rounded: rates down, prefactors up, so that the stated bound is implied by the computed one.

\paragraph{Convergence limits.} For $D=5$, $a_0=4$, $a_1=12$ and $\kappa=1.05$, one has $T_0=208$, $T_1=1008$, $\sigma_w=9$, $h_s=7$ and $H_0=31$. The spanning term converges ($V_1(0)\le0.9$) for $\eps\le1.341\times10^{-3}$, and the chain term ($\min_\lambda r(\lambda,0)\le0.9$) for $\eps\le6.176\times10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{The level of the registered experiment.} At $\eps=5\times10^{-4}$, the level that the experiment of Section~\ref{sec:cert-exp} certifies (with $D=5$, $a_0=4$, $a_1=12$, $R=L-1$), the computation gives $s_1=0.2155565$ and $s_2=0.0351690$ (fraction $1$), with prefactors $C_1=421.6$ and $C_2=9.608\times10^{4}$ at these exact rates, and $C_1(0.2155)=417.81$ and $C_2(0.0351)=91225.8$ at the rates rounded down. The stated prefactors below are at least both, so the stated bound
\[
\Pr(\text{failure})\le4.22\times10^{2}\,n_x\,e^{-0.2155\,(n_y-31)}+9.61\times10^{4}\,n_x\,e^{-0.0351\,(n_y-31)}
\]
is implied by the computed one. At the smaller rate $s_2=0.02$ the same computation gives $C_2=5.36\times10^{3}$, so the bound
\[
\Pr(\text{failure})\le422\,n_x\,e^{-0.2155\,(n_y-31)}+2\times10^{4}\,n_x\,e^{-0.02\,(n_y-31)}
\]
holds for every $L\ge2$, $n_x\ge5$ and $n_y\ge32$, under the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:cert-lemma}; it has the constants of \eqref{eq:ml}. At the certified level $\hat\eps$, the constants of Remark~\ref{rem:sharper} are computed in the same way with the fraction $0.9$.

\subsection{Remarks on the proof}\label{s1:sec:remarks}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The proof never uses that the window corrections are optimal or that stage~1 uses a particular weight; this is why one certificate of stage-1 events serves every admissible $a_1$.
\item Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:A} uses that every edge changes each coordinate by at most one; this is where the square lattice enters.
\item The later stages enter only through Lemma~\ref{s1:lem:X} and the metric facts of Section~\ref{s1:sec:metric}, that is, through the weight bounds $0.98\le w_e\le1.02$.
\item The declared rows and the strips make the end families explicit: residual defects never lie in a declared row, so no landing sum over declared rows is needed.
\end{enumerate}
